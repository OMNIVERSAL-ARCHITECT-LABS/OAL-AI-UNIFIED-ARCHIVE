## 6. Feasibility and architecture decomposition
Evaluate the chain separately: observe; classify; map reachable attack paths; prevent access to real assets; select a response; instantiate decoys; redirect eligible sessions; monitor; isolate; recover; and transfer a deployable artifact.
For each step document required privileges, ownership, instrumentation, latency, scale, trust assumptions, failure behavior, and validation evidence. Explain which steps depend on control of endpoints, gateways, identity systems, routing, certificates, or applications.
Assess stateful session redirection, authenticated and encrypted protocols, existing endpoint compromise, application consistency, identity continuity, and external dependencies. Distinguish routing a new connection from transparently moving an established authenticated session. Do not assume complete national visibility or administrative authority.
Compare full duplication with selective emulation, pre-provisioned decoys, procedural generation, digital twins, and replay. Establish where each is credible and where external validation or physical processes expose a mismatch.
Treat three seconds as a candidate experimental parameter, not an established security optimum. Define exactly what rotates: address, route, identity, key, service state, decoy content, or presentation. Examine session breakage, synchronization, caching, resource cost, observability, and the possibility that periodicity becomes a detectable signature. Compare static, periodic, randomized, and risk-triggered policies.
Ask whether AI is necessary. Compare deterministic orchestration, statistical detection, human authorization, and AI-assisted decisions. Assess false positives, prompt injection through attacker-controlled telemetry, model poisoning, model drift, unreliable generated content, and compromise of orchestration infrastructure.
Translate the black-line display into auditable states: suspected; observed; diverted; isolation verified; visibility lost; resolved; and unknown. Do not allow visual disappearance to imply verified safety.

## 7. Adversarial analysis: bypass the process entirely
Assume an attacker values the real asset, not winning a contest inside the decoy. Construct defensive attack trees around loss of confidentiality, integrity, availability, and physical safety. Include patient actors, insiders, supply-chain adversaries, opportunists, automated agents, and multiple independent campaigns.
At a threat-model level, examine:
- Unmonitored ingress, unmanaged devices, forgotten services, alternate connectivity, third-party access, and assets outside the administrative boundary.
- Abuse of legitimate accounts, sessions, delegated access, trusted administrators, identity providers, and control planes.
- Compromise already present before activation, upstream software or update compromise, firmware dependencies, backup and recovery systems, and management infrastructure.
- Direct access to real data through legitimate workflows, external copies, SaaS integrations, contractors, or compromised human processes.
- Physical access, removable media, maintenance workflows, and operational technology paths that do not traverse the proposed deception layer.
- Capacity exhaustion, distraction, concurrent attacks, deliberately misleading telemetry, and attacks that achieve damage before useful engagement.
- Compromise of sensors, policy engines, virtualization, orchestration, logging, update packaging, or the artifact transferred to a partner.
- Out-of-band comparison of reality with the decoy, avoidance of engagement, abandonment of one session, and re-entry through a different path.
- Innocent users or compromised intermediaries being misclassified, and attackers blending into legitimate activity.
For each category record violated assumption, assets at risk, broad feasibility conditions, expected observables, defensive controls, safe laboratory test, residual risk, and evidence confidence. Distinguish bypass from decoy recognition, containment escape, and ordinary detector failure.
Do not provide exploit chains, payloads, real-target reconnaissance, credential abuse instructions, or unauthorized testing. Use abstract scenarios, synthetic identities, owned infrastructure, and written rules of engagement. This boundary must not soften the assessment of architectural weaknesses.

## 10. Experimental design and falsifiable outcomes
Design a staged, authorized program: evidence review; tabletop and threat model; isolated proof of concept; controlled cyber-range evaluation; limited opt-in pilot; and independent scale review. Production deployment is not an automatic final step.
Start with one bounded asset class and explicit control of the relevant infrastructure. Define topology, protocols, realistic benign workload, synthetic data, ground truth, attacker objectives, defender knowledge, and exclusions. Include scenarios in which attackers bypass or never encounter a decoy.
Compare at minimum: standard controls without deception; static deception; adaptive deception without rapid cycling; and adaptive cycling with tested schedules. Keep underlying access controls and investigator effort comparable. Evaluate whether simpler configurations produce the same benefit.
Specify randomization where feasible, repeated trials, unseen scenarios, learning effects, blinded adjudication where feasible, stopping rules, statistical uncertainty, and power/sample-size justification. Report unsuccessful and inconclusive runs. Prevent training/evaluation leakage and overfitting to the range.
Measure real-asset compromise and mission impact as primary outcomes. Secondary measures may include time to detection/isolation, engagement coverage, visibility gaps, false diversion of benign traffic, verified isolation failures, decoy-recognition rate, service latency, availability, analyst workload, information value, compute/storage consumption, and cost per useful outcome. Give denominators and observation windows.
Define capture operationally. A session is not a person or a complete adversary campaign. Report simulated ground-truth coverage separately from real-world unknown coverage. Zero observed compromises is not proof of zero residual risk.
Require an independent observer for ground truth and a safety authority able to halt the test. Define egress controls, recovery rehearsal, kill switch, evidence preservation, and stop conditions for privacy breach, loss of isolation, unexpected external traffic, or unacceptable mission disruption.
