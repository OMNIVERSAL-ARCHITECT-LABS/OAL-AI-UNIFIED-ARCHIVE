## 3. Methodology: Design Science Research (DSR)

Design Science Research is the gold standard for IS capstone projects that involve building an artifact (framework, model, prototype). It's widely accepted in the information systems discipline and fits perfectly with what UAGC expects.

### The Six Phases (Peffers et al., 2007)

| Phase | What You Do | Your Application |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem Identification | Define the organizational problem | Organizations using multiple AI tools lack governance structures for cross-validation and coordination |
| 2. Objectives for a Solution | Define what a solution should achieve | A framework that improves decision quality, reduces AI bias, and provides audit-ready documentation |
| 3. Design & Development | Build the artifact | Create the Multi-AI Governance Framework (architecture diagram, workflow model, validation protocols, role definitions) |
| 4. Demonstration | Show it works | Apply the framework to 2–3 realistic organizational scenarios (e.g., strategic planning task, risk assessment, vendor evaluation) |
| 5. Evaluation | Measure effectiveness | Compare single-AI vs. multi-AI outputs on accuracy, completeness, and bias detection; survey IT professionals on perceived usefulness |
| 6. Communication | Present findings | Your capstone paper + presentation |

### Supporting Theoretical Frameworks

- **Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)** — To measure perceived usefulness and ease of use of the governance framework among IT professionals
- **TOE Framework (Technology-Organization-Environment)** — To analyze organizational readiness factors for multi-AI adoption
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** — To ground your governance model in established standards
- **Kotter's 8-Step Change Model** — To address the leadership/change management dimension of adopting multi-AI workflows

## 4. Framework Architecture (The Core Artifact)

Your capstone deliverable is the framework itself. Here's a high-level structure:

### Layer 1: AI Platform Selection & Role Assignment
- Define which AI platforms handle which tasks (e.g., research, code review, strategy analysis)
- Establish criteria for platform selection based on task type

### Layer 2: Cross-Validation Protocol
- Define when and how outputs from one AI are verified by another
- Establish consensus thresholds (e.g., 3-of-5 agreement)
- Document adversarial critique procedures

### Layer 3: Governance & Oversight
- Human-in-the-loop decision points
- Escalation protocols for conflicting AI outputs
- Audit trail requirements and documentation standards

### Layer 4: Organizational Integration
- Role definitions (who manages the multi-AI workflow)
- Training and change management plan
- KPI dashboard for monitoring framework effectiveness

### Layer 5: Ethical & Risk Management
- Bias detection mechanisms
- Data privacy controls across platforms
- Compliance mapping (NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act awareness)

## 5. Data Collection Strategy

Since this is a capstone (not a full doctoral dissertation), you have flexibility:

### Primary Data
- **Self-documented case study**: Your own multi-AI workflow logs, prompt strategies, and cross-validation results (anonymized as needed)
- **Scenario testing**: Run 3 identical decision-support tasks through (a) single AI and (b) your multi-AI governance framework, then compare outputs on quality metrics

### Secondary Data (Optional but Strengthening)
- **Survey of IT professionals** (10–20 respondents via LinkedIn or professional networks) using TAM constructs to evaluate the framework's perceived usefulness and adoptability
- **Expert review**: Have 2–3 IT professionals or faculty review your framework and provide structured feedback

### Metrics to Measure
| Metric | How Measured |
|---|---|
| Output accuracy/completeness | Rubric-scored comparison of single-AI vs. multi-AI responses |
| Bias detection rate | Count of biases/errors caught by cross-validation that single-AI missed |
| Decision confidence | Self-reported Likert scale (if using survey) |
| Perceived usefulness (TAM) | Survey instrument |
| Perceived ease of use (TAM) | Survey instrument |
| Time to decision | Clock time for single-AI vs. multi-AI workflow |
