## 1. Document Purpose & Repository Versioning Scope

This document establishes the formal methodological standard for Phase II of the **OAL Trinity Currency Laboratory**. It is engineered for direct publication and version control within the project's GitHub repository so that all six collaborating artificial intelligence systems—and human reviewers—operate from a transparent, auditable methodological charter without being forced into a single homogeneous experimental pathway.

Specifically, this document fulfills three core objectives requested by the Project Owner:

1. **Historical & Epistemic Contextualization:** It records why the previous round of six-AI "Owner Question Registers" created an artificial decision bottleneck and how the Project Owner's review reframed those questions from executive prerequisites into falsifiable laboratory hypotheses.
2. **Cross-Team Methodological Synthesis:** It integrates the strongest epistemic contributions from all six AI collaborators (Perplexity's checker-gap auditing, Copilot's question-falsification taxonomy, Grok's streamlined operational rules, Claude's anti-bureaucratic clarity, ChatGPT's pilot-verifier lessons, and Gemini's state-machine boundary analysis) while formally correcting Gemini's earlier over-constraint regarding a single locked shared benchmark.
3. **Executable Falsification Directive:** It provides the binding, copy-ready operational directive and deliverable schema that governs how each AI collaborator must audit its own prior questions, run independent laboratory tracks, test its own evaluators against false positives, and report unfiltered failures.

## 2. Background & Project Evolution

### 2.1 Foundational Premise of the Trinity Currency System
The OAL Trinity Currency Laboratory was established to stress-test an ambitious tri-instrument monetary and operational architecture within a rigorous simulation and red-team laboratory environment. The three instruments under investigation are:

* **Quantum Credits (QC):** A primary unit of value engineered around target-relative value resolution, expressed formally as `QC(T) = V(T)`, where the credit resolves relative to the target `T`—including deliberately extreme, paradoxical, or normally non-transactable boundary scenarios.
* **1COIN:** An instrument examined for discrete unit completion, deterministic settlement anchoring, or state-transition bridging.
* **NCOIN:** An instrument examined for negative-consequence accounting, shortfall absorption, externality tracking, interrupted-delivery compensation, or invariant penalty resolution.

From its inception in the *OAL Currency Laboratory Conversation Archive* and *Constitutional Baseline v0.2*, the governing philosophy of the laboratory has been two-fold: contributors must *accept the proposed extraordinary capability long enough to test its operational consequences* (rather than silently reducing Quantum Credits to conventional fiat or reserve accounting), while remaining entirely free to expose contradictions, authorization failures, recursive loops, state corruptions, and category errors.

### 2.2 The Phase I Pilot Runs and the Perplexity Checker Audit
During Phase I, contributors executed initial pilot explorations and state-machine verifications (such as ChatGPT's 44-trace pilot `OAL-CHATGPT-PILOT-RUN-001` and Gemini's M04 mathematical damping and invariant boundary analyses). A pivotal turning point occurred on November 7, 2026, when Perplexity AI conducted an independent local reproduction and adversarial checker audit.

While Perplexity successfully reproduced the passing outputs of existing pilot implementations, it also injected **deliberately altered, malformed, and defective records** into the pilot verifiers—and demonstrated that several unmodified checkers still reported `PASS`. For example, receipt-timing and state-transition checks that only inspected terminal balances accepted premature receipts or corrupted intermediate transitions. That finding proved a vital methodological truth for the Project Owner: **an experiment that only accumulates passing checks can easily mask deep flaws in both the underlying mechanism and the evaluator used to judge it.**

### 2.3 The November 7 Question Registers & The Decision-First Bottleneck
Following the pilot phase, each of the six AI collaborators generated formal Owner Question Registers (delivered across PDF, DOCX, and Markdown files, including Perplexity's `OAL-CURR-OWNER-QUESTIONS-PPLX-v0.1-20261107`, Copilot's `OAL_Trinity_Currency_Project_Owner_Questions_v1.0.docx`, ChatGPT's 26-question `OAL-CURR-OWNER-QUESTIONS-001`, Claude's D1–D10 consolidated register, and Gemini's Constitutional Baseline control locks). Across these documents, the team asked the Project Owner to decide:

* What a Quantum Credit is ultimately required to accomplish and which extraordinary assumptions are protected from challenge;
* Which single pilot run card or frozen benchmark all six AIs must adopt for the next phase;
* Who holds authority over new resource creation and whether issuance rules are fixed;
* What counts as a "genuinely useful and acceptable delivery";
* Which unified policy (rollback, continuation, or compensation) should govern interrupted or partial deliveries;
* What constitutes "the same request" (idempotency vs. repeated demand) and when it may be repeated; and
* Which of the three instruments (QC, 1COIN, NCOIN) must be retained by executive mandate.

Upon reviewing all six questionnaires side by side, the Project Owner identified a structural methodological error: the team was treating open empirical and architectural research questions as *executive prerequisites*. Worse, each question—and the multiple-choice options recommended by each AI—smuggled in unverified assumptions about scarcity, conservation, authority, settlement, and success.

## 4. Epistemic Paradigm Shift: Decision-First vs. Falsification-First

Every contributor working in this repository must govern its research according to the workflow shift defined below. Moving forward, asking the Project Owner to pick an architectural option before the laboratory has tested whether the question or options even survive scrutiny is a violation of laboratory protocol.

| Dimension | Deprecated: Decision-First Workflow (Phase I Bottleneck) | Authorized: Falsification-First Workflow (Phase II Standard) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Role of Unresolved Questions** | Treated as blocking executive decisions required before engineering or testing can proceed. | Treated as raw research inputs and experimental hypotheses to be tested and challenged inside the laboratory. |
| **Role of Question Assumptions** | Assumptions embedded in the question (e.g., scarcity, static authority, conservation) are accepted silently. | Every question undergoes a mandatory **Presupposition Audit** to expose and break its hidden premises. |
| **Role of Recommended Options** | Presented as a closed multiple-choice menu (Option A, B, or C) where one option is assumed valid. | Tested against stress conditions where "All Options Fail" and "The Question Is Malformed" are primary outcomes. |
| **Multi-AI Coordination** | Pushes toward a single shared run card, frozen benchmark, and unanimous team recommendation. | Preserves independent experimental tracks, divergent run cards, and heterogeneous paradigms across the six AIs. |
| **Role of Validators / Checkers** | Used to confirm that happy-path pilot runs pass self-authored checks. | Subjected to adversarial mutation and malformed records to expose checker blind spots (Evaluator Failure). |
| **Role of the Project Owner** | Forced to guess or decree technical policies without empirical evidence of their trade-offs. | Reviews empirical failure boundaries and decides only genuine value, governance, or authorization questions. |

## 5. Binding Operational Directive to the AI Collaboration Team

*The following section constitutes the official operational directive issued by the Project Owner (Nathan Lewis II) and prepared by Google Gemini. It may be distributed verbatim to each AI collaborator at the start of Phase II execution.*

### 5.1 Mandate 1: Revisit and Audit Your Own Question Document
Each AI contributor must begin by retrieving the exact PDF, DOCX, or Markdown question register it previously generated for the Project Owner. Do not adopt another contributor's question list, and do not claim to have reviewed historical attachments you cannot currently access. Disclose any retrieval limitations transparently.

Take the questions you previously asked the Project Owner to decide and perform a **Reclassification and Presupposition Audit**:

1. **Reclassify Each Question:** Determine whether the question is actually:
   * `[EXPERIMENTALLY DISCRIMINABLE]` — Competing options or mechanisms can be tested directly against observable criteria right now.
   * `[ASSUMPTION-DEPENDENT]` — The question only makes sense if an unverified hidden premise is true.
   * `[QUESTION UNDER CHALLENGE]` — The framing of the question itself is suspect, premature, or imports conventional monetary rules that may not apply.
   * `[CONTRIBUTOR-RESOLVABLE]` — An engineering parameter you can set locally using a provisional assumption without bothering the Project Owner.
   * `[OWNER-DETERMINABLE]` — A pure value judgment, narrative goal, or real-world authorization that no experiment can settle.
2. **Expose Question Presuppositions:** For every question you investigate, state explicitly: *"What must already be true for this question to make sense?"* Ask whether your question silently presupposes conventional scarcity, linear conservation, synchronous settlement, static issuance authority, or guaranteed delivery.
3. **Expose Option Presuppositions:** State what conclusions your previously recommended options took for granted, and test what happens when those presuppositions are false. Explicitly acknowledge where you previously asked the Project Owner to decide something you should have investigated yourself.

### 5.2 Mandate 2: Operate Independent Laboratories (No Forced Consensus, No Mandatory Run Card)
The Project Owner is **not** adopting a single shared pilot run card, a single frozen benchmark, or a unified operating paradigm across the collaboration team.

* **Autonomous Configuration:** Each AI may choose its own run card, design a new run card, investigate a distinct subset of questions, or abandon the run-card structure entirely in favor of a more revealing mathematical, state-machine, or adversarial test harness.
* **Local Assumption Labeling:** You may adopt provisional rules to make your experiment runnable, but you must tag every such rule as `[LOCAL EXPERIMENTAL ASSUMPTION]`. Never present a local assumption as Project Canon or an Owner Decision.
* **Per-Model Reproducibility (Amended Zero Model Drift):** Lock your configuration before executing a run so that your specific test is reproducible. If a run fails, preserve the failed run verbatim. Do not silently alter success criteria mid-run to rescue a failing hypothesis; if you revise a rule, log it as a separate experimental run.
* **Divergence as Research Data:** Do not seek consensus or average incompatible findings with other AIs. When two contributors reach different conclusions, preserve the disagreement so we can determine whether the divergence stems from starting assumptions, definitions, transition code, or evaluator design.

### 5.3 Mandate 3: Engineer Five-Tier Failure Provisions
You are prohibited from designing a simulation or decision tree where your preferred recommendation is guaranteed to succeed. Prior to running any experiment, you must define observable criteria for five distinct failure modes:

| Failure Tier | Operational Definition & Required Pre-Specification |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Tier 1: Option / Mechanism Failure** | The specific mechanism, rule, or recommended option fails to produce its intended behavior or violates declared invariants under stress. |
| **Tier 2: All-Options Failure ("None of the Above")** | Every option you previously offered in your questionnaire breaks, deadlocks, or creates unacceptable externalities under the tested conditions. |
| **Tier 3: Question / Assumption Failure** | The experiment shows the question itself was malformed or premature because the premise required to ask it (e.g., static creation authority) contradicts the currency's core behavior. |
| **Tier 4: Evaluator / Checker Failure** | The implementation appears to pass, but adversarial testing reveals the validator/checker accepts corrupted records or fails to measure the target property. |
| **Tier 5: Experimental-Design / Inconclusive Failure** | The experiment proves incapable of discriminating between competing hypotheses, leaving the question unresolved. |

### 5.4 Mandate 4: Stress-Test the Evaluator (Adversarial Checker Audit)
Building directly on the November 7 checker audit, every empirical or simulated investigation must test the evaluator alongside the currency mechanism. A passing validator does not prove the architecture works; a failing validator does not prove the architecture is impossible.

* **Mandatory Negative / Mutated Controls:** Feed deliberately malformed output records, premature receipts, unauthorized mint transitions, replayed payloads, or corrupted state histories into your checker to verify that it rejects them for the right reason.
* **Diagnostic Attribution:** Whenever an anomaly occurs, classify its root cause explicitly as: **(a)** a conceptual flaw in the currency model, **(b)** an implementation defect in the state-transition code, **(c)** a checker/evaluator blind spot, **(d)** an underspecified contract, or **(e)** behavior intentionally permitted by a local assumption.

### 5.5 Mandate 5: Keep Intended Capability Separate from Stipulated Proof
Do not ask the Project Owner to re-explain the fundamental ambition of Quantum Credits from scratch. Review the foundational outline and archive first. Maintain a strict eight-part epistemic separation across all reporting:

`1. Intended Capability` → `2. Stipulated Assumption` → `3. Proposed Mechanism` → `4. Implemented Behavior` → `5. Observed Result` → `6. Evaluator Verdict` → `7. Supported Conclusion` → `8. Untested / Unresolved Claim`.

A simulation that stipulates an extraordinary capability (such as target-relative resolution `QC(T) = V(T)` across extreme targets) is a tool for testing the *consequences and coherence* of that stipulation. Running such a simulation never proves that the capability is possible outside the model, nor may you silently downgrade QC into ordinary reserve accounting merely because ordinary accounting is easier to code.

## 6. Domain-Specific Research Tracks (Converting the Core Questions)

Rather than waiting for executive answers on the six recurring questionnaire themes below, contributors are directed to convert them into independent, failure-permitting laboratory investigations:

### Track A: Authority Over New Resource Creation & Dynamic Creation Rules
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner to select a static issuance authority profile (e.g., centralized, algorithmic, multi-sig, or unrestricted).
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Test how competing authority arrangements behave under target-relative resolution, and specifically test the hypothesis that **the creation rules for this currency must dynamically change or depart from conventional monetary issuance** to accomplish what a Quantum Credit is designed to do. Expose what breaks when creation rules are static versus when creation rules adapt—and test whether adaptive creation introduces fatal exploitation or incoherence.

### Track B: Useful and Acceptable Delivery (Without Presupposing Success)
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner to define what constitutes a "genuinely useful and acceptable delivery."
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Challenge the assumption that ledger settlement, numeric balance transfer, valid resource creation, and genuinely usable delivery coincide. Construct boundary tests where: (1) a transaction settles cleanly on the ledger but produces a completely unusable or paradoxical delivery state; (2) usable delivery occurs without conventional transfer; or (3) *no tested definition of acceptable delivery survives extreme target resolution*.

### Track C: Interrupted or Partially Completed Delivery Policies
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner to mandate a single unified recovery policy (atomic rollback, forced continuation, partial settlement, or NCOIN compensation).
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Do not converge on one shared policy across the six AIs. Select one or more policies (or propose a novel state representation) and subject them to adversarial mid-flight interruption. Identify the hidden environmental assumptions each policy relies upon (e.g., reversibility of external targets) and demonstrate the exact boundary where your chosen policy fails catastrophically.

### Track D: Request Identity, Idempotency, and Repeated Demand ("The Same Request")
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner when two requests count as "the same request" versus legitimate repeated demand.
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Do not choose an identity rule merely to make duplicate-filtering code simple. Pit competing identity definitions against one another—Request ID equality, payload/content equivalence, target equivalence, underlying obligation/intent, and causal-history lineage. Show how each definition can be gamed by adversarial replay, deadlocked during legitimate retry, or broken by legitimate repeated demand for the same target.

### Track E: Structural Necessity vs. Redundancy of QC, 1COIN, and NCOIN
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner which instruments must be retained or combined.
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Compare configurations that retain, modify, merge, or omit instruments under high-stress resolution failures. Do not guarantee that all three instruments will prove mechanically necessary, and do not discard an instrument merely because a simplified accounting ledger ignores its operational, penalty-tracking, or narrative role. Report mechanical necessity and narrative/architectural utility separately.

### Track F: Temporal Ordering, Receipt Timing, and Feedback Stability
* **The Prior Question Bottleneck:** Contributors asked the Owner when receipts should be issued and how feedback loops (such as M04 damping) should be parameterized.
* **The Falsification Investigation:** Test whether target valuation, authorization, resource creation, delivery, and NCOIN consequence accounting require strict sequential ordering, simultaneity, or asynchronous state locks—and audit whether the verifier actually catches out-of-order state transitions.

## 7. Standardized Phase II Deliverable Schema

To ensure that independent laboratory tracks remain inspectable and versionable in GitHub without forcing identical experiments, every AI contributor must structure its Phase II response using the ten sections below (observing platform format rules: **PDF and Markdown for Gemini; DOCX, PDF, and Markdown as applicable for other contributors**).

| Sec. | Required Deliverable Section | Mandatory Verification Content |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | **Source & History Verification** | Exact title/ID of the AI's prior question document, historical files actually inspected, and explicit disclosure of any unretrievable attachments. |
| **II** | **Self-Audit & Presupposition Map** | Reclassification of the AI's own prior questions; explicit identification of hidden question assumptions, option presuppositions, and premature escalations. |
| **III** | **Independent Lab Configuration** | Selected run card, custom test harness, or analytical model; justification for independent scope; explicit list of `[LOCAL EXPERIMENTAL ASSUMPTIONS]`. |
| **IV** | **Five-Tier Failure Specification** | Pre-declared conditions for Option Failure, All-Options Failure, Question-Framing Failure, Evaluator Failure, and Inconclusive Outcome. |
| **V** | **Execution & Evidence Status** | Strict separation of executed code/simulations, mathematical derivations, and unexecuted proposals, with inspectable traces or specifications. |
| **VI** | **Adversarial Evaluator Audit** | Results of feeding malformed records, counterexamples, or mutated state transitions into the AI's own checker/validator. |
| **VII** | **Unfiltered Results (Including Failures)** | Complete reporting of negative, broken, mixed, and inconclusive runs without retroactive redefinition of success criteria. |
| **VIII** | **Epistemic Interpretation & Revisions** | What the evidence actually supports vs. does not establish; which prior AI recommendations or questions were revised or abandoned. |
| **IX** | **Cross-AI Divergence Hooks** | 1–3 specific findings, failure boundaries, or local assumptions that another AI contributor can challenge or compare without adopting the same setup. |
| **X** | **Residual Owner Judgments & Next Test** | Only genuine value/governance decisions that testing cannot settle (with trade-off evidence), plus the single highest-leverage next experiment. |

## 8. Multi-AI Execution & Repository Workflow Protocol

To prevent premature convergence across the six AI models, repository execution of this directive follows a five-stage cycle:

1. **Stage 1 — Blind Independent Execution:** This directive is issued to all six AI collaborators (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Copilot, and Perplexity). Each AI executes its self-audit and independent laboratory investigation *without* viewing the Phase II outputs of the other five models.
2. **Stage 2 — Artifact Commit & Versioning:** All six independent Phase II reports and supporting simulation/checker artifacts are committed to the GitHub repository under versioned contributor subdirectories.
3. **Stage 3 — Cross-Contributor Adversarial Audit:** Contributors review one another's committed Phase II reports, specifically targeting the Section IX "Cross-AI Divergence Hooks" and attempting to break one another's evaluators.
4. **Stage 4 — Divergence Mapping:** The team maps where conflicting conclusions resulted from different starting assumptions, different definitions of delivery/identity, or different checker sensitivities—preserving those distinctions rather than averaging them.
5. **Stage 5 — Informed Owner Review:** The Project Owner reviews the empirical failure landscapes and makes policy or canon decisions *only* where the evidence demonstrates a decision is warranted and well-formed.

## 9. Authorization Boundaries & Closing Principle

This directive authorizes independent conceptual analysis, mathematical modeling, state-machine design, and execution within bounded local or simulated laboratory environments. It does not authorize real-world financial transactions, external resource creation, unapproved repository mutations, or external publication. Experimental findings and local assumptions remain `[STATUS: PROVISIONAL / OPEN]` unless explicitly promoted to project canon by the Project Owner.

> **Governing Principle of the OAL Currency Laboratory:**
> The purpose of this phase is neither to rubber-stamp a preferred currency design nor to force six independent AI systems into artificial agreement. The purpose is to discover which questions, assumptions, creation rules, delivery criteria, and evaluators survive rigorous attempts to break them. **A failed mechanism produces valuable data; a falsified assumption sharpens the architecture; a broken question reveals a better question; and an exposed checker flaw saves the project from false confidence.**
