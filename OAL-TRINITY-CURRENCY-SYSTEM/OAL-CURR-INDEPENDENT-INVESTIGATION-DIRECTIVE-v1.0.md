## Background

The OAL Trinity Currency Laboratory is an independent, multi-AI research effort exploring a post-scarcity / target-relative value system centered on Quantum Credits (QC), together with supporting instruments 1COIN and NCOIN. The laboratory operates under the principle that extraordinary capabilities (including target-relative value resolution of the form QC(T) = V(T), even for extreme or normally non-transactable targets) may be stipulated long enough to examine their operational consequences, while remaining fully open to challenge.

In early November 2026, each collaborating AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, and Grok) independently generated a question register (PDF, DOCX, and/or Markdown) consolidating owner questions, recommended options, and next-step proposals. These registers generally framed unresolved architectural, operational, and policy issues as decisions the Project Owner should make before further laboratory work could proceed.

On November 8, 2026, the Project Owner reviewed the collective question sets and issued a methodological clarification. The Owner’s position is that the majority of the questions do not yet warrant an immediate owner selection among the offered options. Instead, they should be converted into independent laboratory investigations designed to expose assumptions, permit the questions themselves and the recommended options to fail, and generate evidence that can later inform (or render unnecessary) an owner decision. The Owner explicitly declined to adopt a single shared run card or benchmark, rejected forced consensus as a success criterion, and emphasized that failure during testing is expected and useful.

This document synthesizes that clarification into a formal, versioned research directive. It incorporates the strongest elements identified across the multi-AI discussion while preserving the Owner’s core requirements of independence, failure-permissiveness, and non-convergence.

## Purpose

This directive exists to:

1. Convert unresolved owner-question registers into independently designed, inspectable laboratory investigations.
2. Require that questions, recommended options, mechanisms, assumptions, and evaluation methods themselves be capable of failing.
3. Establish that consensus, a shared run card, or a unified operating paradigm is not a success criterion for this phase.
4. Provide a stable, versioned reference (suitable for the project GitHub repository) that each collaborating AI can use when designing and reporting its next investigation.
5. Reduce premature decision burden on the Project Owner while still distinguishing genuinely owner-dependent judgments from experimentally investigable questions.

Experimental findings produced under this directive do not automatically become canon or owner-approved policy.

## 1. Methodological Correction

For many of the questions previously posed, the appropriate response is not an immediate owner selection among the offered options. The appropriate response is investigation.

**Insufficient evidence for an informed owner decision is a reason to investigate, not automatically a reason to halt the laboratory.**

Each contributor shall treat its own prior question document as research input rather than as a fixed decision checklist. Uncertainty is to be converted into investigable structure.

## 2. Revisit and Audit Your Own Prior Question Document

Each AI must begin by identifying the specific question register (PDF / DOCX / Markdown) that it previously generated.

- Name the document and state what you were able to re-open or reconstruct.
- Disclose any retrieval limitations honestly.
- Reclassify each consequential question as owner-dependent, experimentally investigable, assumption-dependent, premature, contributor-resolvable, or itself under challenge.
- You are explicitly authorized to conclude that a question you previously presented as requiring an owner decision should instead have been investigated first. That conclusion is a valid research finding.

Do not treat your earlier questionnaire as authoritative merely because you authored it.

## 3. Independent Laboratory Configurations

The Project Owner is not adopting a single pilot run card, shared benchmark, or unified operating paradigm.

Each contributor may independently:

- Select its own run card (or none).
- Design a different experimental configuration.
- Investigate a different subset of questions.
- Propose an alternative approach and explain its purpose.
- Reject the run-card abstraction if another methodology is more informative.

Agreement is permitted when earned by evidence. Unanimity is not a success criterion. Do not force convergence, and do not manufacture disagreement. If results diverge, examine whether the divergence arises from assumptions, definitions, starting conditions, evaluation methods, or evidence. Do not average incompatible conclusions into apparent consensus.

Diversity of experimental pathways is a research asset.

## 4. Mandatory Failure Provisions

Every consequential hypothesis, recommendation, mechanism, policy, or evaluation method must be structurally capable of failing.

For each major claim, explicitly state before execution:

- What would count against it.
- What would expose an inadequacy in its framing.
- What would show a competing approach performs better under the declared conditions.
- What would leave the issue unresolved.
- What findings would cause revision or abandonment.
- How the evaluation method itself could incorrectly report success or failure (evaluator failure).

Valid outcome space must include “none of these options,” “the question is not yet well-formed,” “all recommended options fail,” “assumption failure,” “evaluator failure,” and “insufficient evidence to distinguish.”

If no possible result could change your conclusion, state that limitation rather than presenting the work as a discriminating test.

## 5. Challenge Assumptions and Preserve Ambition Without Guarantee

Review existing project records before asking the Owner to restate the intended capability of a Quantum Credit. The historical starting concept is target-relative value resolution (including extreme or normally non-transactable targets), examined inside a laboratory / simulation context.

Maintain a strict separation among:

1. Intended capability  
2. Assumed / stipulated mechanism  
3. Implemented behavior  
4. Observed result  
5. Supported conclusion  

Do not silently reduce the work to conventional accounting. Do not treat a successful simulation that assumes an extraordinary capability as independent proof that the capability exists outside the model. All operational assumptions and proposed extraordinary capabilities remain open to challenge.

## 6. Example Investigation Areas

These are illustrative directions, not a mandatory common test suite. Each contributor may handle them differently or pursue other questions:

- **Authority over resource creation** — including the possibility that creation rules themselves must change. Distinguish deliberately unrestricted authority from unspecified authority.
- **Useful and acceptable delivery** — test whether displayed balance, correct quantity, valid resource, usable resource, and accepted outcome coincide. Permit outcomes in which delivery is technically complete yet unusable or unacceptable, or in which no satisfactory definition is established.
- **Interrupted or partially completed delivery** — contributors need not adopt a unified policy. Investigate resumption, rollback, compensation, terminal partial completion, or alternative conceptualizations, and identify the conditions under which each fails.
- **Same request / repeated request** — compare identifier, content, intent, obligation, target, and causal-history definitions. Do not select a definition solely for implementation convenience.
- **Roles of Quantum Credits, 1COIN, and NCOIN** — configurations that retain, modify, combine, or omit instruments are permitted. Do not guarantee all three will prove necessary. Report mechanical and narrative/operational value separately.

## 7. Test the Evaluator as Well as the System

Earlier code-audit work demonstrated that checkers can accept deliberately altered or malformed records. Continue that approach where applicable.

Possible methods: deliberately malformed output records, faulty implementations, counterexamples, boundary conditions, alternative evaluation criteria, independent derivation of expected results, mutation-style testing.

Carefully distinguish:

- Defect in the implementation  
- Weakness in the checker  
- Underspecified contract  
- Behavior intentionally permitted by the chosen policy  
- Limitation of the model or experiment  

A passing check does not automatically establish that the checker evaluates the intended property. A failing check does not automatically establish that the broader currency concept is impossible.

## 8. Local Experimental Assumptions

Provisional rules may be adopted to make an experiment executable. Label them explicitly as **[LOCAL EXPERIMENTAL ASSUMPTION]**. They are not owner decisions and are not project canon.

Record the configuration before interpreting results. If a rule, criterion, or implementation is later changed, preserve the original result and treat the revised run as a new, clearly distinguished experiment. Do not silently redefine success to rescue an unsuccessful run.

## 9. Do Not Replace Investigation with Another Owner Questionnaire

Investigate what can responsibly be investigated. Escalate to the Owner only when a genuine owner judgment, consequential authorization, or information that cannot responsibly be assumed is required. For any such escalation, explain why experimentation cannot settle it, what evidence would still inform it, and whether the laboratory can continue without it.

## 10. Required Response Structure

Each independent response should contain:

1. Source review and limitations (identify your earlier question document and access status).  
2. Question-to-investigation map (assumptions, alternatives, methods).  
3. Independent laboratory approach (chosen scope, configuration, and consequential differences from other approaches where known).  
4. Failure provisions (how hypotheses, recommendations, framing, and evaluation method can be challenged).  
5. Evidence and execution status (separate proposed, analytical, actually executed, and untested).  
6. Results (include unfavorable, mixed, and inconclusive findings; supply inspectable artifacts for material claims).  
7. Interpretation and limits.  
8. Targeted next step that addresses the uncertainty exposed by the work.

Do not fabricate results. If a proposed test cannot be executed, supply an executable specification or analytical investigation and label it accurately.

## 11. Authority and Publication Boundaries

This directive authorizes preparation of independent laboratory investigations and execution within available, appropriately bounded local or simulated environments.

It does not authorize real financial activity, external resource creation, repository modifications, publication, or other consequential external actions without separate approval.

## Closing Principle

The purpose of this phase is not to prove that a preferred design works, prove that it cannot work, or produce a unanimous team position.

The purpose is to learn which questions, assumptions, mechanisms, and evaluation methods survive meaningful challenge—and where they fail, remain uncertain, or require revision.

An unsuccessful design can produce a useful investigation. An investigation that cannot distinguish success from failure cannot adequately inform the design.
