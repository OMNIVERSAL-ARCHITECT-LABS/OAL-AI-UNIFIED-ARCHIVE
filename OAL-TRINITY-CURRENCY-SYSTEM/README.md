# OAL Trinity Currency Laboratory — Quantum Credits, 1COIN & NCOIN

This folder contains the evolving research, experimental architecture, collaborative analyses, and formal laboratory documentation for the **OAL Trinity Currency System**, comprising **Quantum Credits (QC), 1COIN, and NCOIN**.

The project began as an exploration of unconventional currency architectures capable of operating beyond ordinary fixed-denomination, reserve-backed, or market-limited monetary assumptions. Through successive independent reviews by the AI Collaboration Team, the work developed into a broader investigation of **target-relative value resolution, settlement, countervalue, transaction boundaries, and the conditions under which currency itself ceases to be a meaningful mechanism**.

The current project status is **PRE-CANON / EXPERIMENTAL**. No currency mechanism, temporal model, settlement architecture, negative-value model, conservation rule, authority structure, or boundary taxonomy should be interpreted as final merely because multiple AI collaborators have proposed or supported it.

The laboratory preserves several foundational distinctions, including **valuation versus ownership, authority, transferability, resolution, settlement, and consequence**. It also maintains the rule that a rejected, impossible, unauthorized, sellerless, non-transferable, or otherwise unsuccessful transaction does **not automatically generate NCOIN**.

Current research tracks include the Static, Dynamic, Commitment, and Relational Quantum Credit models; the question of whether QC evaluates, synchronizes with, supplies, instantiates, or transforms target-relative value; competing interpretations of 1COIN as a settlement executor, record, package, or state; competing NCOIN domains involving liability, consequence, entropy, remediation, and countervalue/conservation; recursive currency behavior; null and incoherent targets; simulation-versus-referent distinctions; and the evolving relationship among the Non-Purchasable Domain, Absolute Transaction Boundary, Ontological Transaction Limit, Quantum Resolution Null Domain, and Currency Event Horizon.

The folder also preserves the **Currency Review Laboratory methodology** itself: experimental baselines, laboratory charters, standardized test records, mechanism test suites, independent AI analyses, red-team challenges, decision records, version histories, boundary exercises, comparative results, and eventual candidates for a Version 2 Trinity Currency Specification.

A central methodological principle governs the work:

**Accept the proposed capability long enough to test its consequences, but do not protect the capability from contradiction, redundancy, failure, or redesign once the premise has been granted.**

Accordingly, the laboratory is permitted to conclude that all three instruments are necessary, that one or more are redundant, that instruments should merge, that an additional mechanism is required, or that the architecture itself requires substantial revision.

The purpose of this repository is therefore not to demonstrate that the Trinity Currency System already works. It is to preserve a transparent, version-controlled research record and build the experimental framework capable of determining **which architectures survive testing, which fail, why they fail, and what evidence should govern the next design decision**.
