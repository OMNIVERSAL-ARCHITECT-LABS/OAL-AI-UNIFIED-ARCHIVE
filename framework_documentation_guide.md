# Framework Documentation Guide
## From Concept to Client-Ready Document

---

## PHASE 1: FRAMEWORK SELECTION

### The First Decision: Which Framework?

You need to pick ONE framework to document first.

**Selection Criteria:**

1. **Which one is most immediately applicable** to someone else's problem?
2. **Which one can you explain** without requiring people to understand your entire cosmology?
3. **Which one have you created the most clarity** around already?

**The Tiebreaker:**

Which one could you explain to a smart 12-year-old in 10 minutes?

That's your starting framework.

---

## PHASE 2: DOCUMENT STRUCTURE

### The Target: 20-30 Page Definitive Guide

**Title Format:** THE [FRAMEWORK NAME]: A Practical Guide

---

### **Section 1: The Problem (2-3 pages)**

**What to include:**
- What problem does this framework solve?
- Who experiences this problem?
- Why existing solutions don't work
- One concrete example of the problem in action

**Writing goal:** Someone should read this and think "Yes, I have this exact problem."

---

### **Section 2: The Core Insight (3-4 pages)**

**What to include:**
- What's the fundamental principle behind your framework?
- Where did this insight come from?
- Why is it different from conventional approaches?
- One visual diagram if possible

**Writing goal:** The "aha!" moment - they understand the unique perspective.

---

### **Section 3: The Framework Itself (8-12 pages)**

**What to include:**
- The actual system, step by step
- Clear terminology definitions
- How to apply it
- Decision trees or process flows
- Examples at each step

**Writing goal:** Complete clarity on how the framework actually works.

---

### **Section 4: Case Studies (4-6 pages)**

**What to include:**
- 2-3 examples of applying the framework
- At least one from YOUR work (proving it works)
- At least one hypothetical but in a different domain (proving it transfers)
- Show the before/after for each

**Writing goal:** Readers see concrete proof the framework delivers results.

---

### **Section 5: Implementation Guide (3-4 pages)**

**What to include:**
- How to start using this today
- Common mistakes
- What success looks like
- What to do when you get stuck

**Writing goal:** Someone finishes reading and knows EXACTLY what to do next.

---

### **Section 6: Advanced Applications (2-3 pages)**

**What to include:**
- Where else this could apply
- Limitations and edge cases
- Future development possibilities

**Writing goal:** Sophisticated readers see the depth; beginners know where to grow.

---

## PHASE 3: DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### **Principle 1: Clarity Over Comprehensiveness**
Don't try to explain everything. Explain THIS framework well.

### **Principle 2: Examples Over Abstraction**
Show don't tell. Every concept gets a concrete example.

### **Principle 3: Utility Over Elegance**
Someone should finish reading and know what to do next.

### **Principle 4: Accessibility Over Sophistication**
Write for smart people who don't know your universe. No jargon without definition.

---

## PHASE 4: FIRST ACTIONS

### Today's Work (2 hours total)

**Step 1 (20 minutes):**
- Write down your candidate frameworks
- Pick one based on selection criteria
- Commit to it

**Step 2 (1 hour):**
- Write a shitty first draft of Section 1 (The Problem)
- Don't edit - just write
- Get words on the page

**Step 3 (30 minutes):**
- Write one concrete example of someone experiencing this problem
- Make it specific: give them a name, context, show what went wrong
- This becomes your opening case study

**Goal:** 3-4 pages answering "What problem does this solve and for whom?"

---

## PHASE 5: WHAT TO DO (AND NOT DO)

### DON'T:
- Start with comprehensive background on your entire cosmology
- Try to explain all your frameworks at once
- Use unexplained jargon or mythology-specific terms
- Write for other AIs or people who already know your work
- Aim for perfection on the first draft

### DO:
- Start with a real problem real people have
- Use concrete examples throughout
- Define every term the first time you use it
- Write for someone smart but unfamiliar
- Accept that draft one will be rough

---

## PHASE 6: PRODUCTION TIMELINE

### **Week 1: The Problem + The Core Insight**
- Draft Sections 1-2
- Focus: Make the problem vivid and the insight clear
- Target: 5-7 pages

### **Week 2: The Framework Itself**
- Draft Section 3
- Focus: Step-by-step clarity
- Target: 8-12 pages

### **Week 3: Case Studies**
- Draft Section 4
- Focus: Concrete proof it works
- Target: 4-6 pages

### **Week 4: Implementation + Advanced**
- Draft Sections 5-6
- Focus: Practical next steps + future possibilities
- Target: 5-7 pages

### **Week 5: Edit and Refine**
- Read through completely
- Add visuals/diagrams
- Tighten language
- Check for consistency

### **Week 6: External Feedback**
- Get 2-3 people outside your circle to read it
- Ask: "Is this clear? Would you use this? What's confusing?"
- Make final revisions

**By end of 6 weeks:** You have a real, complete, usable document.

---

## PHASE 7: DESIGN TOOLS

### **Format**
- Google Docs or Word - nothing fancy yet
- Standard formatting: headings, body text, bullets
- Professional but not over-designed

### **Visuals**
- Hand-drawn diagrams are fine for first draft
- Scan them if needed
- Digital cleanup can come later

### **Tone**
- Professional but conversational
- Like you're explaining to a colleague over coffee
- Authoritative without being condescending

### **Sentence Structure**
- Vary sentence length
- Short ones for emphasis
- Longer ones for explanation and nuance
- Avoid walls of text

---

## PHASE 8: THE CRITICAL QUESTION

Before you start writing, answer this:

**Which framework are you documenting?**

Not "I'm thinking about a few options."
Not "I need to develop it more first."

Just: **"I'm documenting [FRAMEWORK NAME]."**

Write it down. Commit to it.

---

## PHASE 9: AFTER THE DOCUMENT

### What Happens When It's Done?

**Step 1: Identify Potential Clients**
- Who has the problem this solves?
- Make a list of 10 specific people or organizations
- Find contact information

**Step 2: Create Value Proposition**
- One paragraph: "I help [type of person] solve [specific problem] using [your framework]"
- Example: "I help AI training teams create behaviorally consistent models using ontological frameworks that prevent logical contradictions"

**Step 3: Outreach**
- Send the document to your 10 targets
- Offer a free 30-minute consultation
- Goal: Get one paying client

**Step 4: Learn**
- Did they understand it?
- Did they see value?
- What questions did they have?
- Would they pay for implementation help?
