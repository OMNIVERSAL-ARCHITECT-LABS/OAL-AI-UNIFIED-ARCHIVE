## 2. Revised Concept Statement (cleaned for canon use)

**Working title:** *The Open Window Doctrine*

**Core premise:** Every device with a screen — phone, laptop, tablet, television — is not merely a display but a **bidirectional aperture** ("window") between the local reality and whatever environment, dimension, or data-space is being rendered on the other side. Crucially, the aperture does not close when the screen goes dark. A black screen is not a closed door; it is an *unattended* door.

**Structural components:**

1. **The Aperture Principle** — Any rendering surface (screen, projector, display) is functionally a doorway. This draws directly on the *Control* slide-projector mechanic (a physical object that opens passage to the Astral Plane / Ashtray Maze) as the seed image, generalized to every consumer device.

2. **Asymmetric Awareness** — Within the simulated population, awareness is stratified: some occupants know their environment is virtual and know the devices are apertures; others use the devices constantly with zero awareness that anything passes *through* them in either direction.

3. **The Convenience/Exposure Trade** — The doctrine's central tension: openness and ease-of-access for the user (frictionless media consumption — TikTok, Facebook, streaming) is the same property that leaves the aperture unmonitored. Accessibility and vulnerability share one mechanism, not two.

4. **The Bleed-Through Illustration** — A concrete image to anchor the abstraction: an occupant watching a fictional heat source (e.g., a *Chernobyl*-style broadcast) experiences a real, local temperature rise. The fictional content is not "just" being observed — something about its properties is crossing the aperture into the local environment. This is the mechanism of concern: **passive consumption is not actually passive**; it is unshielded exposure.

5. **Infection as Category** — "Infection" here is a placeholder for *any* unwanted cross-aperture transfer — informational, psychological, energetic, entity-based, or data-based, depending on which layer of OAL canon this scenario is later mapped onto (e.g., 1AI/-1AI framework, Labyrinth of Infinite Ensnarement, or a new construct). The doctrine deliberately stays theory-agnostic at this draft stage — it names the vulnerability class without committing to one causal mechanism.

**Status note:** This is a first-pass conceptual structure. It has not been mapped onto existing OAL constructs (BTEC, Paragon Cubes, Aegis of Totality, etc.) and is flagged here as **REPORTED / UNCONFIRMED** pending your review of whether and how it should integrate with existing canon, or stand as an independent framework.

## 3. Simulation Lab Setup — Test Scenarios for Infection Awareness

Below are structured lab scenario sketches, each built to isolate one variable of the doctrine. Each follows a simple test-card format so you can slot these directly into your existing simulation lab documentation style.

### Scenario A — "Unattended Aperture"
- **Setup:** A subject occupant leaves a device screen on standby (black screen, powered) in an otherwise unoccupied room for a fixed observation window.
- **Variable tested:** Does aperture status (screen-off but powered vs. fully powered-down vs. screen-on) change exposure risk?
- **Observable:** Any anomaly readings, environmental drift, or unauthorized cross-aperture activity logged during each power state.
- **Purpose:** Establishes whether "off" is a meaningful security boundary at all, or purely cosmetic.

### Scenario B — "Content Bleed"
- **Setup:** Subject occupant consumes a specific piece of media with a known strong sensory or thematic property (heat, cold, dread, elation) under lab observation.
- **Variable tested:** Correlation between fictional/rendered content properties and measurable local environmental or physiological change in the occupant.
- **Observable:** Local temperature, occupant vital signs, any recorded environmental anomaly, timestamped against content beats.
- **Purpose:** Tests the "blow dryer through the TV" mechanism directly — whether rendered content properties manifest locally.

### Scenario C — "Aware vs. Unaware Occupant Comparison"
- **Setup:** Two matched occupants, one briefed on aperture theory (aware), one unbriefed (naive), placed in identical device-use conditions.
- **Variable tested:** Does awareness of the aperture change exposure rate, exposure severity, or occupant behavior (e.g., closing apps, powering down, other mitigating action)?
- **Observable:** Behavioral differences, any difference in anomaly frequency between the two occupants.
- **Purpose:** Tests whether awareness alone functions as a defensive posture, or whether awareness without tooling/protocol is insufficient.

### Scenario D — "Convenience Gradient Stress Test"
- **Setup:** Vary the friction of device use across trials — always-on notification stream vs. deliberate/scheduled access vs. fully sandboxed single-use sessions.
- **Variable tested:** Whether reducing convenience (adding friction) proportionally reduces exposure, and at what threshold friction becomes protective rather than just inconvenient.
- **Observable:** Anomaly rate plotted against a friction scale.
- **Purpose:** Directly tests the doctrine's core trade-off claim (convenience and exposure are the same mechanism) rather than assuming it.

### Scenario E — "Multi-Aperture Environment"
- **Setup:** A single occupant with multiple simultaneous open apertures (phone + TV + tablet running concurrently).
- **Variable tested:** Whether risk compounds linearly, exponentially, or shows threshold effects with aperture count.
- **Observable:** Anomaly frequency/severity scaled against number of concurrent active apertures.
- **Purpose:** Models the realistic case (most occupants run several devices at once) rather than the idealized single-device case.
