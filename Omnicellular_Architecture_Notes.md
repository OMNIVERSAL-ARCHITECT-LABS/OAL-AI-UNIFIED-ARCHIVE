### How many cells are in the human body?

Scientists estimate that the human body contains on the order of 30–40 trillion human cells, often rounded to "about 37 trillion."[1][2]

More precise recent analyses that break the body down by tissues and cell types find that:
- An average adult male has roughly 36 trillion cells.[3][2]
- An average adult female has roughly 28 trillion cells.[4][3][2]
- Earlier whole‑body estimates for a typical adult generally fall between 30 and 37 trillion cells.[5][6][2][7]

### What is a representative sample of the US population? Like, can you give me figures?

A "representative sample" of the U.S. is one whose makeup closely matches key characteristics of the entire U.S. population (for example, age, sex, race/ethnicity, and region) so that results can be generalized to all adults.[1][2][3]

**What "representative" means:**

- It is a subset of people that reflects the same proportions as the target population on relevant traits (e.g., if the U.S. is about half female, the sample should be about half female too).[1][2][3]
- Researchers typically use probability sampling and then apply weights so the sample lines up with U.S. Census benchmarks (age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, region, etc.).[4][5][6][7]

**Concrete figures for a U.S. adult sample:**

As an illustration, many national polls use about 1,000–2,000 adults, then weight to match Census distributions; here's a simplified target profile for U.S. adults based on 2020 Census–style benchmarks.[5][8][6][9]

**Example: 1,000-person nationally representative sample of U.S. adults**

| Characteristic | Target in population | Example count in 1,000-person sample |
|----------------|---------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Age 18–29 | ≈ 19–20% of adults[8][9] | ~190–200 people |
| Age 30–44 | ≈ 25–26%[8][9] | ~250–260 people |
| Age 45–59 | ≈ 25–26%[8][9] | ~250–260 people |
| Age 60+ | ≈ 29–30%[8][9] | ~290–300 people |

| Men | ≈ 49% of adults[9] | ~490 people |

**Typical sample sizes called "nationally representative":**

- Large national social-science or health surveys often use samples from about 1,000 up to tens of thousands and document that they are "nationally representative" of U.S. adults or households.[5][11][6][7]
- For example, a recent national political poll describes "a nationally representative probability sample of about 1,100 U.S. adults 18+," then weights by gender, age, race/ethnicity, and region to match Census data.[6][7]

### How many cells would be in a representative sample of two thousand adults?

For a representative sample of 2,000 adults, you're looking at roughly **6 × 10¹⁶ human cells** in total — about **60–70 quadrillion cells**.

Here's a concrete way to see it, using recent average cell-count estimates per adult:[1][2][3]
- Average adult has on the order of 30–37 trillion cells.
- Multiply by 2,000 adults:
  - 30 trillion × 2,000 = 60 quadrillion.
  - 37 trillion × 2,000 ≈ 74 quadrillion.

So a reasonable ballpark is about **60–70 quadrillion human cells** in a 2,000‑adult sample, depending on the exact mix of ages, body sizes, and sexes.[1][2][3]
