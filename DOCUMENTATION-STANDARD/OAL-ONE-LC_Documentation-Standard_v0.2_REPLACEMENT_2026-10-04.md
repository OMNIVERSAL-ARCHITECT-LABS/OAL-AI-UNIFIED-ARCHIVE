## Executive Summary

This v0.2 replacement corrects a material interpretation error in the withdrawn ChatGPT v0.1 documentation standard. The governing requirement is architectural: narrative belongs in the narrative body, while tables, charts, figures, diagrams, matrices, row-and-column displays, graphics, and comparable visual/structured representations belong in a designated Exhibits or Appendix area. There is no quantity threshold before that placement rule applies.

The source review confirms that the original commission required every table, chart, graphic, diagram, or figure to be numbered, titled, discussed in the narrative, referenced by item number, and placed in an appropriate designated section or appendix. A separate portion of the source material discussed a possible threshold only for whether a dedicated front-matter List of Tables/List of Figures was needed. That list-index question must not be confused with exhibit placement.

This replacement removes all body-placement exceptions for structured visual content. If a document contains even one substantive exhibit, that exhibit receives a formal identifier, title, purpose, source/derivation note where applicable, a narrative cross-reference, and placement in the designated Exhibits or Appendix structure. The body must remain understandable without requiring the reader to decode the exhibit.

The human coordinator's 2026-10-04 correction and retraction directive is preserved verbatim in Appendix B. At the time of preparation, the coordinator stated that an electronically signed ONESTAMP retraction record was being generated. This document records that statement; it does not independently verify the external signature or GitHub publication state.

## 2. Source Review and Interpretive Finding

The review covered the original documentation-standard commission and the independent versions supplied from Claude, Perplexity, Grok, Microsoft Copilot, and ChatGPT. The comparative assessment is summarized in prose here and in Table E.1 in the Exhibits section.

The original commission was unambiguous on placement: every table, chart, graphic, diagram, or figure must have a unique item number and descriptive title; must be introduced or discussed in the narrative; must be referenced by item number; and must appear in an appropriate designated section or appendix. The source also separately asked whether a List of Tables/List of Figures should have a reasonable front-matter threshold. Those are different requirements.

Claude's independent version follows the placement requirement most directly: it states that tables, charts, matrices, and figures go in the Exhibits section and must not appear in the body. Claude also uses a List of Exhibits and formal cross-references. Its separate exhibit-list thresholds should be reviewed for consistency with the clarified no-threshold presentation philosophy, but they do not create a body-placement exception.

Perplexity's independent version explicitly permits small essential tables near the narrative and rejects a universal ban on body tables. That conflicts with the clarified architecture and requires revision before it can serve as a governing standard.

Grok's independent version places tables, charts, diagrams, and figures in Exhibits but creates an exception when a single narrow list is considered clearer in place. That exception conflicts with the clarified architecture. Grok also establishes a count threshold for when a separate list of exhibits is added. The placement exception requires revision.

Microsoft Copilot correctly requires numbering and cross-reference, but states that large exhibits belong in dedicated sections. That wording leaves smaller exhibits without the same categorical placement requirement and therefore requires revision for prompt fidelity.

ChatGPT v0.1 permitted a narrow table or figure to remain in the body when deemed necessary and mobile-safe, and it added a three-or-more threshold for lists of tables or figures. The body-placement permission was an unauthorized interpretation. This replacement withdraws it completely.

## 3. Retraction and Replacement Rule

A documentation-standard document is noncompliant with this v0.2 replacement when it establishes a numerical threshold that must be reached before tables, charts, figures, diagrams, matrices, graphics, row-and-column displays, or comparable visual/structured content must be moved into a designated exhibit/appendix area.

Under the human coordinator's directive recorded on 2026-10-04, any published documentation-standard version containing such a placement threshold must be reviewed, formally retracted, and regenerated in updated form. This document records that workspace directive as supplied by the coordinator; it does not purport to create legal rights or obligations outside the collaborative workspace.

ChatGPT's v0.1 standard is specifically treated here as withdrawn and replaced by v0.2 because it permitted inline body placement of some tables/figures and therefore did not faithfully preserve the user's intended document architecture.

A threshold governing only whether an additional front-matter index (for example, a List of Exhibits) is displayed is conceptually different from a threshold governing exhibit placement. To eliminate future ambiguity, this v0.2 standard uses no numerical threshold for either rule: if a substantive exhibit exists, it receives designated placement and appears in the List of Exhibits.

## 4. Governing Presentation Principle

Place for everything; everything in its place. The body is a readable narrative. Structured visual material is supporting material and is placed in the document's designated Exhibits section or, where the material is supplementary/technical, in a clearly identified Appendix or Technical Annex.

The body must explain the point in plain language before or while directing the reader to the supporting exhibit. A reader must be able to understand the argument, finding, requirement, or conclusion without decoding a table or graphic.

The standard prohibits using tables as page-layout devices, using row-and-column grids as substitutes for prose, scattering charts or figures throughout narrative pages, or treating a visual as self-explanatory.

## 5. Mandatory Document Architecture

Every substantive official document must include, in a readable single-column structure: title; organization/entity scope; project/program where applicable; document type; version; status; preparer; date and timestamp with timezone; intended audience; purpose and intended result; relationship to other work where applicable; tailored reality/simulation/non-claim boundaries; executive summary; table of contents; numbered narrative sections; next action/outcome; revision note; and format status.

PDF and DOCX must carry page numbers. Markdown uses heading hierarchy and internal navigation rather than artificial pagination.

If any exhibit exists, the document must also include a List of Exhibits and a designated Exhibits section. There is no minimum exhibit count.

## 6. Exhibit Governance - Zero-Threshold Rule

The zero-threshold rule applies from the first substantive exhibit onward. One table is governed. One figure is governed. One chart is governed. Two diagrams are governed. No preparer may delay formal exhibit treatment until an arbitrary count is reached.

Every table, matrix, chart, graph, diagram, figure, graphic, schematic, map, structured row-and-column display, or comparable visual representation must: receive a unique item number; receive a descriptive title/caption; state its purpose; identify source or derivation when applicable; be introduced and interpreted in the narrative; be referenced by item number and designated location; remain understandable through a textual explanation; and appear in the designated Exhibits section or an explicitly identified Appendix/Technical Annex.

Accepted reference style includes: 'Please refer to Table 6.1 in the Exhibits section.' Spatial-only references such as 'the table below,' 'the chart above,' or 'the following figure' are not sufficient because content reflows across PDF, DOCX, and Markdown.

This rule does not mean that documents should be filled with exhibits. An exhibit must earn its existence by communicating a relationship, comparison, dataset, workflow, architecture, measurement, inventory, or other structured content more effectively than prose. If prose is clearer, use prose and do not create the exhibit.

## 7. Narrative Independence Requirement

Narrative content is the primary communication layer. The body must state the subject, reasoning, findings, decisions, limitations, and implications in prose appropriate to the intended audience.

Exhibits support verification, lookup, comparison, reproduction, or deeper inspection. They do not carry the only copy of a conclusion, requirement, warning, or decision.

For mixed audiences, use layered communication: executive summary; plain-language narrative; detailed technical explanation; exhibits; then appendices/supporting data.

## 8. Mobile-First and Accessibility Rules

Assume that many readers will use a phone in portrait orientation. Use one primary text column, descriptive headings, manageable paragraphs, readable type, adequate spacing, high contrast, and no meaning that depends on color alone.

Because structured visual content is moved out of the narrative body, the body should not require horizontal scrolling. Exhibits that are too wide for a phone must be split, restacked, simplified, or supplied as a technical annex/companion dataset with a complete prose interpretation.

Meaningful figures require text alternatives where the format supports them. Tables require clear headers and units. Accessibility is evaluated on exported artifacts, not only on source files.

## 9. Purpose, Classification, and Claim Boundaries

Documentation exists to accomplish something: inform, define, decide, record, specify, test, preserve, review, execute, or support another explicit purpose. A document whose only result is that another file exists should be reconsidered.

Every document must distinguish lifecycle/maturity status from content nature. Terms such as DRAFT, PROVISIONAL, UNDER REVIEW, APPROVED, RATIFIED, SUPERSEDED, and RETIRED describe lifecycle. Terms such as SIMULATION, HYPOTHETICAL, FICTIONAL, CONCEPTUAL, OBSERVED, CALCULATED, ESTIMATED, or MIXED describe content/evidence nature.

A reader should never have to infer whether a number was observed, sourced, calculated, estimated, simulated, assumed, synthetic, or speculative.

## 10. Required Formats and Fidelity

The baseline publication package for substantive project documents is PDF, DOCX, and Markdown. The three files are different representations of the same substantive document, not independent rewrites.

Titles, version, status, section numbering, conclusions, dates, transcript content, exhibit numbering, source notes, and major claims must remain synchronized across formats.

PDF is the stable presentation copy. DOCX is the editable office copy. Markdown is the repository-friendly plain-text copy. A project may designate one as canonical, but canonical status must be explicit.

## 11. Format Failure and Recovery

A preparer must never claim a required file exists when it does not. If PDF, DOCX, or Markdown cannot be generated, validated, or delivered, identify the missing format; state the actual failure; deliver every successful format; preserve a complete conversion-ready source; explain whether completeness was checked; and provide a recovery path.

The publication status becomes FORMAT INCOMPLETE until the missing artifact is produced or an authorized exception is recorded.

## 12. Preparer Accountability and Multi-AI Independence

The preparer line is part of the record. An AI that materially prepares a document identifies itself. Human direction and AI preparation should not be conflated.

Uniform presentation does not require uniform thought. Collaborators may disagree. Multi-AI documents must preserve material dissent rather than manufacture consensus.

A preparer is accountable for prompt fidelity as well as visual polish. Passing a render check does not prove structural compliance. Structural compliance must be reviewed separately before publication.

## 13. Publication Acceptance Gate

Before publication, verify purpose, audience, organization/entity scope, status, preparer, timestamp, version, claim boundaries, narrative readability, exhibit placement, exhibit numbering, exhibit cross-references, source/derivation labels, mobile usability, accessibility, cross-format fidelity, file existence, related-work references, outcome/next action, and revision status.

The gate fails if any structured visual appears in the narrative body; if an exhibit is unnumbered, untitled, uncited, or lacks a designated home; if the argument depends on the exhibit alone; if a quantity threshold is used to postpone designated exhibit placement; or if required formats are missing without the recovery protocol.

A render may pass while the document fails this gate. Layout correctness and governance compliance are separate checks.

## 14. Versioning, Retraction, and Supersession

Draft/provisional versions use v0.x. A later v0.x may replace an earlier draft when requirements are corrected. The first formally adopted standard may become v1.0 only through authorized human governance.

Retracted or superseded files should remain discoverable as historical records when repository practice allows, but must be clearly labeled RETRACTED or SUPERSEDED and point to the replacement. They must not remain indexed as the current governing standard.

This document is v0.2 because it materially changes the exhibit-placement rule and records the retraction directive affecting v0.1.

## 16. Independent Comparative Conclusion

Claude's implementation is the closest match to the clarified placement architecture because it makes Exhibits the exclusive home for tables, charts, matrices, and figures and keeps prose primary. That structural decision should be preserved.

Perplexity provides strong governance, source, accessibility, and publication controls, but its allowance for small essential body tables conflicts with the clarified placement rule. Grok is concise and operational but creates a single-narrow-list body exception. Copilot captures numbering and cross-reference but limits dedicated exhibit placement to 'large' exhibits. ChatGPT v0.1 created the broadest exception and therefore required replacement.

The synthesis adopted here is not a vote tally. It uses the original instruction and the coordinator's subsequent clarification as controlling requirements, then borrows compatible governance mechanisms from the independent versions.
