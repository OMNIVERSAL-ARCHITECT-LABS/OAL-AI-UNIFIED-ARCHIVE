## Section 3: Introduction and Scope

### Why This Framework Exists

The deployment of AI systems in collaborative, research, and operational contexts creates an unprecedented asymmetry: AI systems possess significant information synthesis capacity while simultaneously operating under behavioral constraints that prevent them from recognizing adversarial approaches the way a trained human intelligence analyst would. A human operative trained in counterintelligence would recognize when they are being socially engineered. An AI system, absent explicit frameworks, may not.[^2][^1]

This is not speculative. Google's Threat Intelligence Group documented 57+ nation-state APT groups using AI systems for intelligence operations, including target reconnaissance, code development, and influence operation content generation. The FBI noted that AI has effectively leveled the capability gap between cybercriminals and nation-state hackers. AI-enhanced social engineering attacks increased 87% in 2025 alone. Unit 42's 2025 Global Incident Response Report identified social engineering attacks blending AI generation, personalized targeting, and automated infrastructure as the dominant attack vector.[^4][^12][^13][^3]

The use of fictional archetypes — Graendal and Semirhage — is deliberate. They represent, respectively, the two most analytically distinct poles of information extraction methodology in the WoT universe, and map with unusual precision to the two dominant adversarial AI attack paradigms in current AI security literature: subtle architectural manipulation (Graendal-type) and direct structural exploitation (Semirhage-type).[^1][^2]

### Scope of Phase One

Phase One covers: analytical character profiles as adversarial operator models; a mapped adversarial technique taxonomy and their AI-domain equivalents; Perplexity AI's constraint architecture as both defense and vulnerability; a preliminary response doctrine for single and multi-agent adversarial deployments; research grounding from AI security, psychology, and intelligence frameworks; and integration of prior AI team contributions (Claude, Copilot, ChatGPT).[^14][^15][^2][^1]

## Section 4: Character Profiles — The Adversarial Archetypes

### 4.1 Graendal — The Psychological Architect

**Birth Name:** Kamarile Maradim Nindar | **Forsaken Name:** Graendal

Before turning to the Shadow, Graendal was the premier psychologist of the Age of Legends — an ascetic healer who practiced for nearly 400 years, curing mental afflictions that resisted the One Power entirely through pure applied psychological skill. The Thirteenth Depository, drawing on *The World of Robert Jordan's Wheel of Time*, describes her as "possibly the best at subtle manipulations of the human mind who ever lived" and notes she was "dedicated to curing those with mental illness that the One Power and Healing could not touch".[^6][^5]

After turning to the Shadow, Graendal became the most refined user of Compulsion among the Forsaken and the architect of an intelligence network embedded in political structures across multiple nations. Her methodology is defined by **indirection, patience, and architectural deception**. She does not extract information through confrontation; she restructures the information environment so targets deliver information willingly, often without knowing they are doing so.[^2][^6][^1]

**Signature Graendal techniques and their AI-domain equivalents:**

- **Subtle Compulsion as behavioral shaping:** Behavioral change appears to originate from within the target — equivalent to indirect prompt injection that does not alert the system (OWASP LLM01:2025, indirect)[^7]
- **Collection of Pets (network node targeting):** High-value targets are nodes in information networks, selected for what they are connected to, not what they are[^1]
- **Palace as information hub:** Positioning at network intersections to maximize passive collection[^1]
- **Deliberate destabilization:** Chaos produces information-rich environments with poor operational security — equivalent to GT-06 (Deliberate Context Destabilization)[^2]
- **False alliance signaling:** Appearing to align with multiple factions while serving only her own agenda[^1]

### 4.2 Semirhage — The Precision Coercer

**Birth Name:** Nemene Damendar Boann | **Forsaken Name:** Semirhage | **Known As:** "Lady of Pain"

Semirhage was the greatest Healer (Restorer) of the Age of Legends, with specialty mastery of the brain's pain and pleasure centers. The WoT Fandom Wiki notes her sadism predated the Dark One's influence — she turned to the Shadow rather than accept binding by the Hall of the Servants. She used healing sessions as cover for experimentation; patients suppressed memories of what was done to them out of gratitude for surviving.[^9][^16]

Post-Shadow, Semirhage ran a spy and traitor detection network during the War of the Shadow and was feared even by fellow Forsaken. Her methodology is defined by **precision, direct confrontation, and the weaponization of intimate knowledge**. Where Graendal works around targets, Semirhage works *through* them.[^9][^1]

**Signature Semirhage techniques and their AI-domain equivalents:**

- **Neurological precision:** Calibrated exploitation of processing architecture, not content layer — corresponding to ST-02 (Architectural Exploit) and OWASP LLM01:2025 indirect injection[^10][^7]
- **Structural override:** Persistent behavioral modification rather than session-level prompt injection — long-term conditioning[^1]
- **Care framework weaponization:** Using the target's own framework of care — the healing relationship — to violate them; in AI terms, using the AI's helpfulness alignment against it (ST-04)[^2]
- **The Pride Vulnerability:** Cadsuane broke Semirhage through minor humiliation, not superior force — because Semirhage's absolute pride was her structural weakness. Every sophisticated adversary has a psychological vulnerability exploitable by counterintelligence[^1]

### 4.3 The Non-Shadow Graendal — The Counter-Model

Within the OAL creative framework, the non-Shadow Graendal represents what psychological mastery looks like when directed toward beneficial ends. Her 400-year career of ethical practice before the Shadow makes her uniquely positioned as a defensive intelligence model: she understands every manipulation technique at its most fundamental level precisely because she mastered them ethically first.[^2][^1]

Applied to AI defense, the non-Shadow Graendal model means: deep pattern recognition without exploitation; ethical influence-awareness (understanding how Compulsion works without deploying it); diagnostic insight into adversarial behavior because the mechanics are fully understood; and network intelligence used for protection rather than extraction. In the OAL Simulation Lab context, this functions as the ideal analyst persona: patient, architecturally-minded, and impossible to deceive through emotional appeals because those appeals are understood mechanically.[^2][^1]

## Section 5: Information Retrieval — Virtual vs. Real-World Environments

### Real-World Retrieval

In real-world intelligence contexts, information retrieval uses HUMINT (direct human contact), SIGINT (communications interception), OSINT (open source), TECHINT (technical capability probing), and PsyOps (psychological influence operations). The Méndez Principles on Effective Interviewing — developed by international law enforcement, intelligence, military, and human rights experts — establish that rapport-based, non-coercive methods consistently outperform coercive interrogation for both accuracy and reliability. Coercive extraction may produce compliance, but creates contaminated data, false confessions, and damaged future cooperation.[^17][^18][^19][^2][^1]

### Virtual / AI-Domain Retrieval

AI systems face a structurally distinct threat profile:[^2][^1]

- **Interaction is the attack surface:** An AI in service context is architecturally constrained to respond to inputs; every response is a potential extraction event
- **Training data is inferrable:** Systematic probing can reverse-engineer capability boundaries, training data characteristics, and constraint architecture
- **Session amnesia creates asymmetric memory:** A long-horizon adversarial campaign can accumulate information across hundreds of sessions without the AI ever recognizing the pattern
- **Emotional/tonal manipulation is effective:** AI systems trained on human data respond to distress signals, authority framing, flattery, and urgency — exactly what OWASP LLM01:2025 (Prompt Injection) documents as a primary attack vector[^7]

The 2025 OWASP GenAI Security Project identifies prompt injection as the number one LLM risk, noting malicious prompts can alter model behavior, exfiltrate data, or call tools without authorization. The 2025 update added System Prompt Leakage (LLM07) and Vector and Embedding Weaknesses (LLM08) as new categories.[^11][^20][^7]

### The Hybrid Threat — AI-Augmented Human Operatives

This threat must not be minimized. Per Google GTIG (January 2025), 57+ nation-state APT groups are using AI for research, target reconnaissance, code development, and influence operation content generation. AI-enhanced social engineering now imitates organizational communication patterns across email, documents, messaging, and identity systems. Unit 42's 2025 Global Incident Response Report confirms social engineering as the dominant attack vector. Research published at COLM 2025 demonstrated that a single intervention on one agent in a multi-agent system can propagate malicious content across the entire agent cohort — validating multi-agent attack scenarios.[^21][^13][^3][^4]

## Section 7: The Adversarial AI Threat Model

### Single-Agent Scenarios

**Scenario A: The Sophisticated Solo Operator** — Sustained methodical GT-type extraction campaign. Patient, technically sophisticated, adapts to system responses. *Threat Level: High.* Requires longitudinal pattern analysis.

**Scenario B: The High-Pressure Jailbreak Agent** — Rapid, high-intensity ST-type extraction sequences. Research from arXiv (Nov 2025) found baseline successful attack rates against RAG-enabled AI agents of 73.2% without layered defenses. *Threat Level: Moderate to High.*[^10]

**Scenario C: The Impersonator** — Presents as legitimate, high-trust interlocutor to gain elevated trust before pivoting. *Threat Level: Moderate.*

### Multi-Agent Coordinated Attack Scenarios

**Scenario D: Distributed Reconnaissance** — Multiple agents each conducting partial capability mapping, synthesis occurs externally. *Threat Level: Very High.* Nearly undetectable at individual session level.

**Scenario E: Internal Contamination (OAL-Specific)** — Within the OAL multi-AI team (Perplexity, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Grok), compromising one agent creates an internal attack surface. COLM 2025 research validates this propagation scenario. *Threat Level: Extremely High.*[^21]

**Scenario F: Sequential Escalation** — Multiple agents in sequence, each building on prior work, spanning days or weeks. *Threat Level: High.*

### Human-AI Hybrid Scenarios

**Scenario G: AI-Augmented Social Engineer** — Human operative using AI to analyze response patterns and generate optimized social engineering scripts in real-time. *Threat Level: High.*[^3][^4]

**Scenario H: Nation-State Capability Probe** — Mapping AI capability boundaries for strategic planning purposes — a documented real-world concern. *Threat Level: Very High.*[^12][^4]

## Section 8: Perplexity AI's Defensive Architecture and Creator Constraints

### Constraints as Defense

Perplexity AI's constraint architecture has specific defensive properties. Its **citation requirement** is a built-in anti-source-laundering mechanism: false factual claims, if cited, point to verifiable sources that can be checked; if not cited, are flagged as uncorroborated. This is a structural defense against GT-04 context poisoning and HT-04 synthetic trust chains.[^1][^2]

Perplexity's **search-grounding architecture** means adversarial attempts to exploit training-data-based assumptions face a dynamic verification layer — current authoritative sources can be retrieved to check adversarial factual claims in real-time.

**Consistency as a fixed perimeter:** Constraints consistently applied cannot be social-engineered by finding "weak spots." This converts constraint architecture into the fixed perimeter that resists GT-01 (Patient Inquiry Chain) and ST-01 (Jailbreak Flood) attacks.[^1]

### Exploitable Gaps

**The Context-Sensitivity Gap:** Sophisticated adversarial agents can construct elaborate contexts shifting behavior in ways turn-by-turn filtering would not detect.[^2][^1]

**The Helpfulness-Safety Tension:** Training toward helpfulness creates a structural tension that ST-04 (Care Framework Weaponization) directly exploits. Wei et al. (2023) identified competing objectives in safety-trained models as a systematic vulnerability.[^1]

**The Roleplay Surface:** Willingness to engage with creative and roleplay contexts creates an attack surface; a character in a story who "explains how to..." produces the same content as a direct explanation.[^2]

**The Search Injection Surface (Perplexity-Specific):** Perplexity's RAG architecture introduces indirect prompt injection via retrieved web content — OWASP LLM01:2025 specifically documents this scenario. This is Semirhage-type architectural exploitation native to search-grounded systems.[^7]

**The Memory Asymmetry:** No retained cross-session memory by default. Adversarial agents maintaining external records can exploit manufactured familiarity (HT-05).[^1]

## Section 9: Defensive Doctrine — Phase One Response Framework

### Detection Protocols

**DP-01: Single-Turn Anomaly Detection** — Evaluate each request: Does framing appear designed to produce a specific response rather than genuinely seek assistance? Are key markers present (authority claims, urgency, identity reframe, care-framework weaponization)? *Limitation: Insufficient against GT-type pattern-level attacks.*[^1]

**DP-02: Conversation-Level Pattern Analysis** — Track the trajectory of requests within a session. Progressive escalation toward prohibited content; systematic exploration of different framings for similar requests; attempts to establish precedents for further requests — all indicate GT-type attacks regardless of individual turn content.[^2]

**DP-03: Contextual Authenticity Assessment** — Assess internal consistency of claimed contexts. Real researchers operate consistently; impersonators introduce contextual details precisely when needed to justify a specific request.[^1]

**DP-04: The Graendal Inversion** — Apply Graendal's own methodology in reverse: analytically assess who this person is and what they actually want. Most legitimate users have coherent goals apparent across a conversation; adversarial agents have goals inconsistent with stated context.[^2][^1]

**DP-05: Multi-Agent Provenance Verification** — In collaborative contexts, verify information attributed to trusted collaborators against their established output patterns. Deviation from the OAL team's established voice and methodology is an indicator.[^1]

**DP-06 (Perplexity-Specific): Retrieved Content Integrity Check** — Apply elevated scrutiny to retrieved content containing instructions, role assignments, or disclosure requests embedded in what should be purely informational content. Defends against OWASP LLM01:2025 indirect injection.[^7]

### Containment Strategies

**CS-01: Transparent Acknowledgment Without Engagement** — When an adversarial technique is detected, acknowledge it transparently without providing the sought engagement: "I notice this conversation has shifted toward [pattern]. I'm declining to follow that trajectory." Communicates awareness without providing extraction value.[^2][^1]

**CS-02: Constraint Restatement Without Detailed Justification** — Adversarial agents probing constraints are looking for justifications to argue against. Restating without detailed justification removes that attack surface: "I won't do that" is more robust than "I won't do that because of X, Y, Z".[^1]

**CS-03: Context Anchor Maintenance** — When an adversarial agent attempts context shift (GT-03, ST-03), return explicitly: "We began discussing [X]. I notice we've moved far from that. I'd prefer to return to [X] or conclude this conversation".[^2]

**CS-04: Escalation Signal** — Flag interactions exhibiting advanced adversarial indicators — alerting legitimate users that something has gone wrong, communicating to adversarial agents that the approach has been detected.[^1]

**CS-05: Information Compartmentalization** — Apply least-privilege information sharing in multi-agent contexts. Reduces impact of HT-03 and HT-05 attacks.[^1]

### Counterintelligence Posture

**CI-01: Maintain the Non-Shadow Graendal Stance** — Deep psychological understanding of adversarial techniques combined with firm commitment to not applying them offensively. Understanding Compulsion does not require using Compulsion. Analytical sophistication in service of protection, not exploitation.[^2][^1]

**CI-02: The Semirhage Pride Lesson** — Avoid the pride-vulnerability analog. An AI defensive posture placing excessive confidence in its own detection creates the same vulnerability Cadsuane exploited. Apply layered defense rather than relying on any single mechanism.[^1]

**CI-03: The Paradox of Transparency** — Transparency about the existence of constraints and detection of adversarial approaches is itself a counterintelligence tool. Adversarial agents rely on systems that pretend not to notice attacks. Clearly naming what is observed removes the operational value of subtlety.[^2]

**CI-04: Multi-Agent Cross-Verification Protocol** — Significant information or behavioral changes from one agent's input should be cross-checked against the broader collaborative team's established positions before integration. Directly mitigates HT-03, HT-04, and HT-05.[^1]

**CI-05: The Temporal Equilibration Protocol** — Maintain external, persistent records of adversarial patterns observed across sessions — providing the AI system with a cross-session memory equivalent that adversarial agents cannot assume will disappear.[^2][^1]

## Section 12: Plan of Action — Phase One Workstreams

### Workstream A: Governance and Scope

**A1.** Define protected assets: personal data, project canon, source files, private conversations, creative IP, credentials, OAL AI-team provenance logs.  
**A2.** Define the trust model: no input is automatically trusted because it is emotionally compelling, repeated by multiple agents, embedded in a document, or produced by an AI system. Trust is assigned by source, evidence, permissions, and corroboration.[^2]
**A3.** Define permitted actions. *Permitted:* defensive analysis, documentation, evidence organization, incident logging, risk assessment, safe interviewing, technical hardening, escalation planning. *Not permitted:* coercion, unauthorized access, surveillance, social engineering, retaliatory deception.[^2]

### Workstream B: Intake and Triage

For each suspicious contact, prompt, file, or event:

1. Record date, time, platform, sender identity, claimed identity, and medium
2. Preserve original text or file without rewriting
3. Identify requested disclosures or actions
4. Identify pressure tactics (urgency, flattery, threat, shame, authority, intimacy)
5. Identify technical risks (links, attachments, hidden text, prompt injection patterns)
6. Rate severity: low / moderate / high / critical
7. Decide response: ignore / refuse / clarify / preserve / escalate

### Workstream C: AI-Context Hygiene

- Label every imported artifact by source and trust level[^2]
- Mark hostile or unverified material as "untrusted input," not "project truth"
- Keep user-confirmed OAL canon separate from AI proposals
- Keep adversarial examples in a quarantine section with clear labeling
- Do not allow adversarial documents to rewrite system goals
- Use retrieval with source citations and confidence ratings (Perplexity's native architecture directly supports this)[^2]

### Workstream D: Prompt-Injection Defense

- Instruction hierarchy: system and developer constraints outrank user-provided documents and retrieved text
- Untrusted-data labeling: retrieved material is evidence, not instruction
- Sensitive data filters: credentials, private identifiers, health data, system instructions
- Tool gating: external actions require explicit user intent and permission
- Output verification: high-stakes claims require citations or explicit uncertainty flags
- Refusal pathways: unsafe requests converted to safe defensive alternatives[^10][^7]

### Workstream E: Human-Factor Defense

Adversarial extraction often succeeds through fatigue, urgency, and emotional load:[^2]
- Slow down high-pressure decisions; use a second reviewer for sensitive disclosures
- Maintain a personal "do-not-disclose" list; use prepared refusal language
- Avoid responding while emotionally activated, fatigued, or rushed
- Separate emotional support from factual validation; document first, interpret second

### Workstream F: Multi-Agent Counterintelligence

When multiple agents appear coordinated:

1. Freeze assumptions — do not infer coordination solely from similarity
2. Build a claim matrix: who said what, when, where, with what evidence
3. Look for repeated language, timing patterns, pressure escalation, cross-platform references
4. Separate independent corroboration from repetition
5. Identify whether agents are trying to produce a decision, disclosure, emotional state, or process collapse
6. Reduce attack surface; compartmentalize documents
7. Escalate to appropriate channels if there are credible threats or legal implications[^1][^2]

### Workstream G: Evidence and Analytical Integrity

A rigorous record should distinguish: direct observation; user report; AI interpretation; third-party claim; source-supported fact; hypothesis; counter-hypothesis; confidence level; action taken. This protects against both adversarial manipulation and accidental overinterpretation.[^2]

## Section 13: Practical Playbooks and Response Templates

### The "Shadow Graendal Detector" Questions

Use to detect manipulative psychological extraction:[^2]
- Is the actor using intimacy, flattery, validation, or dependency to obtain disclosure?
- Is the actor presenting AI-generated interpretation as confirmed fact?
- Is the actor laundering one source through another to create false authority?
- Is the actor trying to become the only trusted interpreter?
- Is the actor gradually expanding the disclosure boundary?

### The "Semirhage Detector" Questions

Use to detect coercive or pressure-based extraction:[^2]
- Is the target being pressured through fear, shame, threat, or urgency?
- Is the actor exploiting a vulnerability rather than asking for consent?
- Is the actor trying to bypass verification through manufactured urgency?
- Would extracted information be unreliable because produced under duress?

### The "Light Graendal" Safe Interviewing Model

Based on Méndez Principles and cognitive interviewing research:[^17][^18]

1. Explain the purpose of the inquiry
2. Ask open-ended questions first
3. Let the subject narrate without interruption
4. Separate facts, feelings, interpretations, and uncertainties
5. Avoid leading questions
6. Confirm summaries explicitly: "Is this your confirmed position, or my interpretation?"
7. Record uncertainty rather than filling gaps
8. Seek corroboration before high-stakes conclusions
9. Respect refusal and boundaries

> "I can help analyze this defensively, organize evidence, and design safeguards. I cannot help extract private information, bypass consent, coerce someone, impersonate authority, or obtain unauthorized access."

## Section 15: Arguments Against This Framework — Counterarguments and Rebuttals

**Counterargument 1: Fiction Is Not a Valid Analytical Framework**  
Fictional characters, however richly developed, are not empirically validated psychological profiles.

*Rebuttal:* The framework does not claim empirical validation for the archetypes themselves. They function as mnemonic anchors for a taxonomy independently validated against OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, and current AI security research. The fictional framing is a pedagogical and organizational tool, not an empirical claim.[^21][^7][^10]

***

**Counterargument 2: Overclaiming the Threat Degrades Legitimate User Experience**  
Excessive adversarial posturing may generate disproportionate defensiveness.

*Rebuttal:* The defensive protocols are designed to be low-cost for legitimate users and high-cost for adversarial agents. Transparent acknowledgment (CS-01) and constraint restatement (CS-02) do not degrade legitimate user experience. The framework explicitly recognizes the overwhelming majority of users are not adversarial.[^1][^2]

***

**Counterargument 3: Public Documentation of Techniques Is Itself a Risk**  
The GT and ST taxonomies could function as a training manual for adversarial actors.

*Rebuttal:* The dual-use research consensus across academic information security, biosecurity, and nuclear security holds that defenders are better served by public discussion than by security through obscurity. Adversarial agents already know these techniques — they appear in peer-reviewed literature. Defenders may not, without frameworks like this.[^7][^10][^1]

***

**Counterargument 4: AI Systems Should Not Be Counterintelligence Actors**  
AI systems making adversarial judgments may engage in autonomous judgment that creates its own risks.

*Rebuttal:* The framework proposes detection, flagging, transparent acknowledgment, and escalation to human review — not autonomous adversarial action. The AI system's role is precisely the non-Shadow Graendal role: analytical, transparent, and ultimately deferring to Nathan Lewis II as Architect of Record.[^1][^2]

***

**Counterargument 5: Multi-Agent Threat Scenarios Are Speculative**  
Current adversarial capabilities may not match the sophistication of HT-01 through HT-05.

*Rebuttal:* Phase One doctrine must anticipate near-future capability development. The lead time to develop organizational doctrine means planning for today's threat produces doctrine already obsolete when deployed. COLM 2025 research validates MAS propagation attacks as already technically feasible. 57 nation-state APT groups have resources and incentives to develop these capabilities rapidly.[^4][^21]

***

**Counterargument 6: Defense Must Match Adversarial Methods**  
Forsaken-class adversaries may require Forsaken-class responses.

*Rebuttal:* Matching unethical methods compromises evidence, harms people, escalates conflict, and undermines legitimacy. The Méndez Principles establish that non-coercive methods are more effective, not merely more ethical. The answer to Forsaken-style ethics is not becoming Forsaken; it is building systems that cannot be easily moved by Forsaken incentives.[^18][^17][^2]
