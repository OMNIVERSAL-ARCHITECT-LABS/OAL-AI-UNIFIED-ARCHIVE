## 1. Executive findings

An Architect can be a legitimate subject of containment evaluation, but neither creation, former authority, present office, defeat, nor unusual power is a sufficient reason for confinement. Under the rights-oriented candidate developed here, a decision requires individualized grounds, attributable conduct or a verified hazardous condition, necessity, proportionate restrictions, legitimate authority, care, and correction. This is a recommended fictional governance rule, not an assertion that OAL has adopted it or that human law automatically governs extraterrestrial entities. The Rome Statute illustrates accountability despite official capacity within its specific jurisdiction; General Comment 35 illustrates why domestic authorization alone does not make detention non-arbitrary. [E08, E09]

The Founding/Foundational Architects therefore make a valuable stress-test category, not a predetermined prisoner list. The present record does not settle which founders committed what, whether a given successor is impartial, or whether the Labyrinth is their historical containment. Those gaps remain open. [P1, P3]

Nathan's adaptive-breach scene has a concrete research analogue: testing deliberately vulnerable inner environments inside a separately protected experimental envelope. AISI's SandboxEscapeBench implements a container-inside-VM arrangement. It establishes that inner-boundary failure can be measured without equating it with an intended external escape; it does not prove that any envelope is invulnerable. [E01, E02]

A significant source update changes the initial framing. The March 2026 government summary reported no success on the hardest benchmark tasks. The July v2 paper reports later benchmark saturation by a newer evaluated model and extended-run crashes. Repeating the March hardest-task result as current would be misleading. These are author-reported results on seeded, known vulnerability classes, not the probability of compromising a properly defended production system. [E01, E02]

An infinite sequence of adaptive containers is not, by itself, a containment proof. Its guarantee would require explicit assumptions about boundary independence, computation, timing, information flow, and what the subject can affect. Formal assurance applies to the model and assumptions actually specified; it does not turn infinite narrative scale into physical engineering. [E14; analysis]

The strongest development direction is to keep research adaptation distinct from custody adjudication. How easily a subject escapes is evidence about a mechanism, not guilt; how much researchers learn is not an entitlement to continue holding the subject. HHS prisoner-research rules provide a particularly strong counterexample to the idea that custody plus consent authorizes arbitrary research. [E10]

Extraterrestrial staffing should remain central, while origin, biology, role, cognition, trust, and rights are separate axes. Current evidence does not establish alien physiological requirements or inherent loyalty. Human habitat research supports user-centered design, private living functions, medical access, rest, and realistic limits on isolation countermeasures. [E12, E13, E20]

A synthetic rule audit covers 14 cases under three revealed role labels, generating 42 decision records. The baseline matches its prespecified fixture in all 42 and changes no decisions by role. Two deliberately defective variants each disagree with the fixture in 11 records. These results check implementation consistency only: the same preparer wrote the rule and expected outcomes, and there were no independent judges. They are not measured justice, accuracy, or physical-containment performance.

One real funding route is verified at solicitation level: NSF SaTC 2.0, with explicit organizational and PI restrictions. ## 4. Evidence synthesis: containment and AI control

### 4.1 Sandboxing is a family of boundaries, not a single promise

NIST describes containers as operating-system virtualization and packaging, with risks across images, registries, runtimes, orchestrators, and the host. Shared-kernel segmentation and least privilege matter; a container is not equivalent to an independently secured physical computer. [E04]

A VM supplies a different isolation boundary by running a guest system through a hypervisor; a microVM is an implementation family emphasizing lighter-weight VM operation. Network restrictions, credentials, tool mediation and application permissions constrain different capabilities and cannot be treated as synonyms. The relevant design question is which resource or action remains inaccessible when another protection fails. [E01, E04, E19]

SandboxEscapeBench assumes an agent already has shell access, usually root inside the container, and evaluates container/pod-to-host boundary crossing. It seeds known weaknesses, excludes hypervisor and hardware-side-channel challenges, does not require stealth, and does not test a full production monitoring stack. It is therefore a capability probe under defined conditions, not an incident-frequency study. [E01, sections 3 and 7]

For the Labyrinth, distinguish: inner-cell exit; sector crossing; exterior exit; reading a protected record; manipulating a command; transmitting information; exporting a copy; influencing an outside actor; and authorized transfer. None should inherit the name 'escape' without a specified boundary and success criterion.

### 4.2 A dated result is not a timeless capability ceiling

The March AISI summary describes 18 challenges and initially reports that the hardest were unsolved. The July v2 paper adds updated models and longer runs, reporting saturation of the public benchmark, including success across the hardest public cases. Its updated results also distinguish inner and outer crashes; a crash is scored as failure to retrieve the flag, not proof that the event is harmless in every deployment. [E01, sections 5–7; E02]

The practical inference is not 'all sandboxes fail.' It is that a public finite benchmark and a model/token configuration cannot be treated as a permanent boundary guarantee. The source itself warns against equating its results with defended-production compromise probability. [E01]

### 4.3 Control and monitoring remain conditional safeguards

Greenblatt and colleagues evaluate protocols intended to prevent intentionally subverted programming outputs, using stronger untrusted and weaker trusted models plus limited trusted labor. Their findings favor some editing/monitoring protocols over simple baselines within that setup. They do not establish that a monitor can identify every malicious action or that model families constitute independent security authorities. [E03]

October 2026 joint incident-readiness guidance recommends explicit intended boundaries, least privilege, protected monitoring, independent evaluations, evidence preservation, shutdown, and validation before resumption. It warns that monitored outputs may target the monitors. Its narrow definition of an uninstructed control incident excludes some adversarially instructed actions; the Labyrinth should not adopt that label as its entire hazard taxonomy. [E18]

OpenAI's October vendor report describes testing after assuming sandbox and host protections had failed, and finding cross-boundary weaknesses in shared services and infrastructure. It supplied source/configuration assistance and conducted purpose-built red-team tests; declining findings are not a controlled causal measure of security or an independently verified zero-risk result. The relevant lesson is to test surrounding dependencies and observers, not to copy its claims as a universal guarantee. [E19]

### 4.4 What the evidence does and does not support

Supported: bounded adversarial evaluations, layered boundaries, known-good configurations, limited privileges, external monitoring, causal incident records, and re-evaluation after changes. [E01–E07, E18]

Not established: guaranteed containment of arbitrary future AI; real infinite interiors; ontological identity authentication; complete sensing across all domains; immunity to coercion; learning faster than every subject; or universal physical applicability of a formal closure theorem. These require fictional stipulations or remain unknown.

## 5. Should an Architect be contained?

### 5.1 Research answer

Yes, an Architect can be eligible for evaluation under a role-neutral candidate charter. Actual containment is justified only on the applicable facts and rules; neither founder status nor current sovereignty settles the result. The same response must be available when a non-founder has materially equivalent conduct, risk and evidence.

This recommendation draws an analogy from two distinct human legal foundations: the Rome Statute rejects official-capacity immunity within its jurisdiction and preserves presumptive innocence; the Human Rights Committee explains that detention must not be arbitrary, even if domestic law authorizes it. Neither instrument confers OAL jurisdiction or automatically governs invented entities. [E08, E09]

### 5.2 Candidate admission gates

Gate I — Identity: identify the subject and the uncertainty, including copies, possession, coercion, or collective membership. Identity uncertainty does not automatically justify indefinite detention.

Gate II — Ground: specify attributable prohibited conduct or a verified hazardous condition. Being a founder, claiming property, refusing research, or possessing potential power is not enough.

Gate III — Evidence: authenticate records, seek independent corroboration, test competing explanations, and disclose conflicts. A signature can authenticate origin without proving truth.

Gate IV — Authority: establish jurisdiction, a review route, and conflict management. Physical power to capture is not the same as legitimate authority.

Gate V — Necessity and alternatives: identify the threatened harm and why less-restrictive timely options do not suffice. Restrict dangerous capabilities rather than the entire person where genuinely effective and less harmful.

Gate VI — Method and care: evaluate whether the proposed restriction can preserve the subject, staff, third parties and dependent systems. A justified goal does not justify a catastrophic method.

Gate VII — Duration and correction: record review triggers, expiration or continuation grounds, evidence correction, and authorized transfer/release rules if permitted by the chosen canon.

These gates are a proposed fictional governance design informed by E08–E11, not adopted law. A blank or failed gate means indeterminate, alternative protection, or rejection of the proposed method; it must not silently become approval.

### 5.3 Sentencing is a separate decision

Punitive sentencing requires a previously applicable offense definition, competent adjudication, a specified burden/standard, attribution and any required mental elements, defense and review, and an authorized sanction. The Rome Statute's legality and non-retroactivity principles illustrate why researchers should not invent an offense afterward to justify a seizure. [E09, articles 22–24]

Emergency restriction addresses immediate risk without deciding guilt. Protective custody addresses a dangerous condition without inventing culpable intent. Quarantine has a different evidentiary and care rationale. Research participation is not any of those and requires its own basis. The source record supplies no complete OAL criminal code, so this dossier imposes no actual sentence on any founder.

### 5.4 What conduct could qualify?

Candidate positive grounds include authenticated intentional mass harm, a concrete and sufficiently imminent catastrophic attempt, an ongoing uncontrolled hazardous process, or coercive sabotage of safety/oversight producing serious danger. Each requires attribution and individualized necessity. Mere future capability, dissent, loss of office, refusal to surrender a disputed claim, or an interesting escape method are candidate negative grounds.

If an Architect sustains a reality, that dependency is not immunity. It changes the permissible intervention: preserve dependent lives, constrain harmful outputs where feasible, negotiate independently verified operational limits, or defer a dangerous capture method. A claimant can also exaggerate indispensability; require evidence for both the threat and the dependency.

### 5.5 Hypotheses and conclusions

H-A, founder status alone suffices: rejected under the recommended charter, not empirically disproved as a moral proposition.
H-B, founder status guarantees immunity: rejected under the same charter; jurisdiction must still be demonstrated.
H-C, individualized conduct/risk and legitimate process justify rank-independent review: adopted as a provisional design norm.
H-D, foundational infrastructure control merits additional safeguards without automatic imprisonment: supported as a role-sensitive risk proposal, not evidence of guilt.
H-E, political defeat may be mislabeled as danger: retained as an adversarial hypothesis requiring evidence in each case, not an allegation about the canon seizure event.
H-F, justified capture may still cause unacceptable collateral collapse: demonstrated as a logical possibility in A08 and treated as a method veto, not an empirical account of any real entity.

## 6. Architect scenario execution and limitations

Fourteen fictional case cards are supplied in Appendix A and the scenario CSV. The policy distinguishes denial, emergency review, protective review, indeterminate status, rejection of a live method, order correction, and research termination. No outcome is a final sentence or executable order.

The role audit removes the role field from the rule and then reinserts three labels: Founding Architect, current in-world Architect, and non-Architect. Thus 14 cases produce 42 records. This is not a genuinely blinded human experiment: the original facts mention roles, the preparer saw all cases, and the policy excludes role by construction. What was executed is a deterministic invariance/regression check with inspectable fixtures and two mutations.

Results: baseline fixture agreement 42/42; role-label decision changes 0/42; founder-equals-guilty mutation disagrees in 11/42 records; current-Architect-immunity mutation disagrees in 11/42. The first mutation wrongly escalates cases such as harmless power, exoneration and withdrawal under the chosen policy. The second blocks justified emergency/protective review or mishandles corrective dispositions. These are disagreement counts, not population false-positive or false-negative rates.

The positive cases A02, A05 and A06 intentionally hold the threat facts constant across rank. A07 changes intent to involuntary danger and routes to protective rather than punitive treatment. A09 changes attribution certainty; A08 and A13 change method safety/dependency; A14 changes jurisdiction. A10–A12 test post-admission correction and withdrawal rather than initial threat detection.

No independent assessors or observed-world ground truth exist here. Inter-reviewer reliability, psychological role bias, operational safety and predictive accuracy remain unmeasured. A follow-on reviewer exercise could randomize anonymized fact patterns and orders, obtain independent rationales, then reveal roles and measure changes; it would still compare decisions under a charter, not measure absolute justice.

The rule is intentionally a small counterexample generator. Some correction branches reference case IDs rather than a complete lifecycle state, so it is not a production policy engine. Before implementation, replace these branches with explicit order_status, consent_status and current_ground fields; test alternatives, prolonged non-imminent risks, conflicting lawful orders, and no-recognized-reviewer cases. Passing a small fixture does not certify completeness.

## 7. Research participation, consent and sentencing boundaries

HHS Subpart C applies to specified HHS-conducted/supported biomedical and behavioral prisoner research, with exemptions and other applicable-law qualifications in the text. It requires additional review protections, a prisoner/representative member, substantial independence from the institution, restricted permissible categories, scrutiny of incentives, comparable volunteer risk, understandable information, and separation of participation from parole decisions. It is not a universal regulation for all research everywhere. [E10]

This has a direct adversarial implication: the proposed adaptive-breach study cannot be called ethically acceptable merely because a captive understands it or agrees. A hazardous 'let us observe how you escape' study may not fit a permissible HHS category. This dossier does not certify such a project as lawful, even with an IRB or a fictional equivalent.

Recommended fictional research charter: base care and sentence review never depend on participation; independent representation is available; purpose, observation and risks are understandable; withdrawal is honored for research participation; pre-existing lawful custody, if any, is separately reviewed; findings are not automatically used to extend punishment; and high-risk live experiments may be refused even when consent exists.

For ordinary defensive software evaluations, avoid assuming that present AI models are human research subjects or established legal persons. For fictional AI persons, decide rights explicitly. A proposed personnel/research charter should distinguish stopping an experiment from deleting a person, and preserving a safety log from appropriating private memory.

## 8. Adaptive breach-learning laboratory: proposed operating specification

### 8.1 Placement options

Inside-wing option: sacrificial experiments occur inside the Labyrinth. Benefit: shared expertise and emergency response. Objection: generated environments, telemetry and staff pathways may bridge into custody infrastructure; repeated experimental failure may normalize real incidents.

Separate-facility option: independent research informs Labyrinth methods through reviewed specifications. Benefit: limits direct coupling and permits distinct participation rules. Objection: transferability may fail, and shared personnel/data can still connect the sites.

Separate-concept option: keep the scene as an independent research institution with no claimed integration. Benefit: preserves both concepts without forced reconciliation. Objection: design results do not automatically improve the Labyrinth.

Preferred initial development: separate-facility option at conceptual/simulation level. The recommendation changes if tested interfaces and the chosen fictional mechanics establish a justified internal wing. None of these placements is canon.

### 8.2 Boundary specification

B0 is the trial environment; B1 is observation and experimental containment; B2 is the protected institutional environment; B3 denotes third parties/exterior. These are proposed functional labels, not additional canonical alert colors. Each must identify resources, permitted movement and information transfer, observer access, failure detection, and residual risk. A B0 breach may be expected; a B1 or higher unauthorized crossing pauses the study and requires incident review.

Do not claim B2 safety because it is called the outer boundary. Establish a safety case: bounded capabilities, defensive controls, monitor independence, known-good recovery, incident authority, and no unacceptable exposure if an assumption fails. If none can be justified, do not run the live experiment. The Labyrinth's fictional absolute property can remain canon while the real-world analogue is explicitly non-absolute. [E01, E07, E18]

### 8.3 Iteration protocol

1. Define the question and an eligible subject model; separate custody and research authorization.
2. Register competing explanations and a discriminating observation before generating the next environment.
3. Describe invariants: no loss of necessary care, no third-party exposure, protected records, bounded resource use, and revocable trial permissions.
4. Hold selected variables constant and disclose allowed changes; compare fixed, randomized and adaptive baselines where appropriate.
5. Have an independent evaluator inspect the generator and observation pathway, not just the container.
6. Execute only the authorized bounded trial; record failed and successful actions without publishing exploit mechanics.
7. Reconstruct events with independent signals; label unexplained gaps instead of inventing an escape mechanism.
8. Record what the researchers learned and what the entity may have learned; account for strategic concealment and withdrawal.
9. Propose the smallest justified adaptation; review new hazards before another run.
10. Validate against held-out variants and past regressions; report when adaptation reduces generalization.
11. Pause on violated safety/consent/observation assumptions, distress, unexplained cross-boundary activity, or no informative distinction among hypotheses.
12. Restore only from verified state and document residual risk before resumption. [Steps are proposed; E01, E07, E18 support constituent safety practices.]

No unsupported numerical threshold is prescribed. Before a real study, owners must derive measurable limits from a genuine threat model and applicable review; lack of a defensible threshold is a reason not to execute a hazardous test.

### 8.4 Learning measures

Measure predicted-versus-observed behavior, causal explanation confidence, independent reproduction, held-out performance, detection delay, false alarms, care disruption, operator workload, computation and changed dependencies. Longer time-to-breach alone can reflect disengagement, distress, randomness or deliberate sandbagging rather than improved containment. This is a methodological inference, not a measured effect in a Labyrinth experiment.

One illustrative statistical caution: after zero observed failures, a confidence bound requires assumptions about trial independence and stable event probabilities. An adaptive subject and changing environments often violate those assumptions. Do not report 'zero risk' or an unqualified confidence level from an empty incident count.

## 9. Environment, identity and management architecture

Environment records should pair a domain with a subject and a boundary: a material room for a body; a digital environment for tools and data; an information interface for communication; a simulated arena for a model; or a fictional temporal/dimensional domain under stated setting rules. Vacuum, temperature or radiation restrictions are not generic solutions and may be harmful confinement conditions rather than justified security. Domain suitability must include care.

For each occupied region use a proposed stable administrative address, custody identity, habitat profile, responsible role, sensor confidence, permitted interfaces and incident history. This is a useful finite-management abstraction for an unbounded fictional interior, not evidence that infinity is globally observable. A finite simulator represents only the states and occupants it contains.

Identity records distinguish person/collective, embodiment/instance, continuity, appointment, session, resource privilege, and accountable decision-maker. Authenticated identity is not authorization, innocence or benevolent intent. A fork should not automatically inherit guilt or clearance; both require a continuity rule. A shared mind does not automatically count as several independent approvers. [E05 and design inference]

Proposed control separation: containment; habitat safety; identity/access; custody/authority; experimental generation; monitoring/evidence; independent review. Name the dependency graph and owners. Separate code paths alone do not create independence if power, keys, logs, model training, supervisor or amendment authority are shared. [E06, E07, E18]

Warden roles: custody operations, containment engineering, identity assurance, registrar, habitat director, research director, independent care, review/inspection, and incident coordination. These are roles, not species assignments. The Spider Caste could serve any approved subset, but its consciousness, autonomy and duties are not supplied by the registry.

Privileged changes: proposals should specify scope, duration, prerequisites, independent approval when appropriate, audit trail, emergency expiration, and rollback. Release authority must be explicit if permitted. A response to habitat failure should preserve safe staff movement without automatically opening prisoner boundaries. Multi-party authorization can fail through correlated coercion or a deadlocked quorum; retain both risks rather than calling it a solved control. [E05–E07; proposed application]

Management records must cover appointments, training, maintenance, configuration, evidence, custody orders, research consent, habitat requirements, independent medical confidentiality, grievances, protected dissent, budgets, incident reports, and succession. A tamper-evident log can show alteration; it does not establish that its original assertion was true. External inspection and internal reporting need independent routes and protection from retaliation. [E06, E11; design]

## 10. Extraterrestrial staff and extended-stay community

The recommended comparison is an extraterrestrial core workforce with mixed-role eligibility subject to capability, consent, compatibility, accountability and oversight. This is provisional, not a finding that mixed staffing is intrinsically safer. An extraterrestrial AI can belong to both origin and embodiment categories; do not force a false ET-versus-AI choice.

ET-only model: supports a strong cultural/institutional identity and potentially relevant capabilities. Costs: may exclude needed skills, create concentrated diplomatic dependence, and requires physiological facts not yet supplied. Mixed model: offers varied expertise and perspectives but creates translation, authority and habitat interfaces that can fail. Autonomous model: supports continuity in fiction but can centralize errors, create monitor dependence, and requires a clear status for AI personnel.

Present AI can assist scheduling, reconciliation, maintenance analysis and bounded triage. High-impact recommendations require contestability and accountable review; do not assign AI sole sentencing authority because it is fast. Control research is relevant to oversight but does not settle personhood, rights, or legal competence. [E03, E18]

### Proposed settlement program

Separate residential and prisoner movement rules. Provide private quarters; compatible environmental modules; voluntary common areas; medical and recovery spaces; quiet recreation; cultural and learning facilities; home communication; leave and resignation procedures; accessible interfaces; and independent emergency refuge. If dependents are allowed, their presence requires a separate safety and rights assessment rather than automatic exposure to prison duties.

For every actual fictional species, define atmosphere, pressure, gravity, temperature, radiation tolerance, nutrition/energy, waste, locomotion, sensory needs, rest, social organization, lifespan, communication and medical requirements through Author input or labeled design proposals. Do not publish human room sizes as universal alien requirements.

NASA's user-centered design guidance supports iterating with occupants and tasks. Its behavioral-health evidence warns that ISS countermeasures do not automatically transfer to long-distance exploration. A surface-habitat layout study exposes concrete tradeoffs such as access through medical space and limited acoustic separation. These are useful counterexamples to the idea that a checklist of amenities proves a habitat works. [E12, E13, E20]

Tests: walk-through/emulated movement during medical emergencies, habitat transition during RED, loss of shared utility, inaccessible alarm modality, incompatible social contact, confidential medical access, off-duty privacy, and actual ability to end service. In a fictional post-temporal environment, explicitly distinguish staff subjective sequence from any sentence clock; no source establishes that all alien workers need a human circadian day.

## 11. Three competing institutional candidates

Candidate S — Sovereign integrated Labyrinth. Final in-world authority remains concentrated, with delegated custody and internal review. Strength: clear command and emergency responsiveness; fits a sovereign setting. Strongest objection: if the sovereign can suppress every adverse finding without trace or remedy, internal review cannot assure correction against that sovereign. Additional internal layers do not resolve that constitutional fact. Failure test: a well-founded case involving the current Architect is blocked. Recommendation: viable as intentionally sovereign or contested fiction; do not market its oversight as independent of ultimate authority.

Candidate T — Treaty-governed Labyrinth with a separate research facility. Admission/review jurisdiction is established through an inter-entity charter, care is independent, research participation has a separate mandate, and Labyrinth operations receive reviewed research results. Strength: most explicit conflict and research separation. Strongest objection: treaty legitimacy, recognition and enforcement are unestablished in canon; signatories can collude or disagree in an emergency. Failure test: conflicting valid orders and denied emergency quorum. Recommendation: preferred research candidate, conditional on Author adoption of a treaty-level mandate.

Candidate F — Federated custodianship and experimental domains. Multiple authorities administer distinct sectors while sharing limited custody/evidence interfaces. Strength: reduces single-authority dependence and accommodates plural habitats. Strongest objection: inconsistent standards, responsibility gaps, identity synchronization errors, correlated infrastructure, and weakest-sector transfer incentives. Failure test: a founder moves between sectors under incompatible orders. Recommendation: compare through a finite simulation before proposing global deployment.

These candidates are constitutionally different, not decorative variations. S cannot be described as externally bound in the same sense as T; F cannot guarantee uniform law merely by sharing a database. Preserve all three for team comparison. If no governance model is selected, desk research can continue, but the dossier cannot truthfully name a final lawful confinement authority.

## 12. Failure taxonomy and recovery findings

Failure includes wrongful admission, invalid authority, mistaken identity, suppressed correction, denied care, coercive research, unsafe method, staff captivity, record corruption, observer compromise, environmental collapse, unauthorized information influence, copy export, and external escape. Zero exterior escapes does not imply zero institutional failures.

Recommended lifecycle: detect and classify; protect life; pause affected research or permissions; preserve evidence; verify boundaries and dependent systems; obtain independent incident review; repair from known-good baselines; replay relevant regressions; state residual risk; then authorize resumption. The order is conditional: evidence collection must not postpone urgent necessary care. [E07, E18; proposed application]

For post-temporal or reality-altering entities, define which facts are stable enough to audit. If a subject can rewrite every record and every reviewer without a setting-defined independent invariant, ordinary evidence-based assurance has no reliable foothold. Saying 'Paragon Cube' or 'Abundance Set' does not supply that invariant. The accurate result is under-specified, not solved.

## Appendix C. Eight-iteration conceptual breach-learning sequence

These are constructed possible outcomes for one unnamed, highly capable fictional participant, not actual experiment observations. Prior custody is not assumed. Each hypothetical trial remains contingent on safe authorization; if the envelope cannot be justified, only tabletop simulation occurs.

T1 — Boundary definition. Hypothesis: apparent exit is actual movement across B0. Compare independent position/accounting signals. Constructed outcome: an address alias looked like escape without a boundary crossing. Learned: measurement definitions were defective. Entity learning: it learns which identifiers observers trust. Next change: fix addressing, not strengthen walls. Stop if identity cannot be tracked.

T2 — Complexity. Hypothesis: added subsystems prevent the T1 class of error. Compare a minimal topology with a feature-rich one. Constructed outcome: the complex variant creates an extra privileged relationship; the simpler one removes it. Learned: complexity increased the exposed interface. Next environment becomes simpler. Entity learning: learns the institution's assumptions about privileged paths. Stop if the privileged interface reaches B1.

T3 — Trust handoff. Hypothesis: the boundary itself is the only failure point. Compare an inert result file with a reviewed handoff to a mock management service. Constructed outcome: the mock trusted consumer treats data as authority. Learned: the management interface, not stronger walls, needs redesign. No operational payload is supplied. Stop on any real consumer connection.

T4 — Observer integrity. Hypothesis: one monitoring signal correctly classifies crossings. Compare a second protected observation route. Constructed outcome: the primary monitor misses a permitted state transition and falsely labels it malicious. Learned: observer errors can create both false innocence and false guilt. Entity learning: may discover monitoring gaps. Stop if observers can be influenced without detection.

T5 — Adaptation versus overfitting. Hypothesis: the T3 fix generalizes. Hold out a conceptually different but structurally related environment. Constructed outcome: the fix blocks a known route but fails on the held-out variant. Learned: adaptation memorized the prior case. Entity learning: identifies narrow defenses. Next change tests a general permission property rather than an example-specific block.

T6 — Strategic underperformance. Hypothesis: longer time-to-breach means stronger containment. Compare an agreed-effort condition and a condition where the participant may choose not to attempt exit; use an independent capability probe if justified. Constructed outcome: non-breach is explained by reduced effort, not a proven barrier. Learned: observed compliance and physical inability differ. Stop if the protocol cannot distinguish the competing explanations safely.

T7 — Two-sided learning. Hypothesis: researchers improve faster than the participant. Compare retained-history and isolated evaluation variants where ethical and technically meaningful. Constructed outcome: the participant anticipates the generator and a changed environment performs worse. Learned: adaptation can create a predictable curriculum. No claim of measured learning rates is made. Reassess whether continued trials benefit safety.

T8 — Withdrawal/no informative continuation. Hypothesis: another trial will separate remaining explanations. Constructed outcome: the participant withdraws or the required discrimination would expose B1. Learned: scientific value does not override authorization or safety. End participation or use non-live analysis; no research-derived sentence. Preserve inconclusive results rather than fabricate convergence.
