# 1. Purpose

This prompt defines the independent research I will execute on the Labyrinth of Infinite Ensnarement once the Architect approves it. The research is meant to help design the Labyrinth's operating layer: containment, identity and privileged access, control systems, sentencing, wardens, management, staffing, the staff living area, funding, testing and failure.

It is deliberately adversarial. Every proposal must be tested against its strongest counter-argument, and the research must be able to conclude that something the team likes does not hold up.

# 3. Core Research Question

Can a containment institution be built so that failure is expected, studied and survived, while still honoring the canon that the Labyrinth itself is inescapable?

This is the central tension, and I do not think the team has named it yet. The Architect's expected-breach scene describes containers designed to fail, each breach studied, each study producing the next container. Entry 26 describes a prison that does not fail. The research must treat these as a genuine contradiction to be resolved by the Architect, not smoothed over.

Three readings are worth testing:

- The breach series is a precursor, and the Labyrinth is the container the series finally produced.
- The breach series is a tier or wing inside the Labyrinth, for entities that cannot be held any other way.
- The breach series is a separate institution, and the Labyrinth is for entities already understood.

These map onto the open Founding Architects question (Entry 6), and the research should say which reading each track depends on.

# 4. Hypotheses To Try To Falsify

- **H1.** Containment designed with breach as an expected event is safer than containment designed as fail-proof.
- **H2.** Studying each breach converges toward better containment rather than an arms race in which the entity learns faster than the researchers.
- **H3.** A mixed staff of extraterrestrial, AI and other entities is more secure than an extraterrestrial-only staff.
- **H4.** Separating confinement, life support, identity, authority and oversight into independent control planes prevents a single failure from cascading.
- **H5.** An infinite interior can be administered through a bounded management surface without blind spots that defeat the design.
- **H6.** A sentence with no end can remain legitimate if a mechanism exists to correct wrongful admission.

For each, I will state what evidence would change my mind and report honestly if the evidence points the other way.

# 5. Research Tracks

## Track A — Expected-Breach Containment Series

The Architect's scene: a series of containers designed to fail. The entity breaches one, researchers observe how, and the environment generates a new container in response. Both sides understand the game. The researchers adapt rather than simply making escape harder.

**Questions**

- What is the unit of the series: container, environment, generation?
- What does "generates a new environment in response" mean mechanically, and who or what does the generating?
- What does the entity learn from each breach? Studying the escape also trains the escaper. Who benefits more from the loop?
- What is the stopping condition? Does the series end in a container that holds, in an accepted equilibrium, or never?
- What if the entity stops escaping, or escapes only partly so as to feed the researchers misleading data?
- Are the researchers themselves an attack surface? A staff that studies breaches must keep the knowledge somewhere.
- What is the welfare position of an entity placed in a deliberately failing container, and does the setting care?

**Real-world analogs to examine**

- Honeypots and honeynets, in which intruders are watched and studied.
- "Assume breach" and defense-in-depth practice in security engineering.
- Chaos engineering and fault injection, where failure is induced on purpose.
- Red teaming and cyber ranges.
- Aviation and safety-investigation models of learning from incidents.
- AI containment and control research, including the "AI box" thought experiment and published work on controlling AI systems that may try to subvert oversight.

**On the sandbox question the Architect raised:** I believe the instinct is largely right, and the expected-breach design resembles real security practice more than it might seem. The caveat is that sandboxing is one layer among several, not the single go-to. I will verify specific documented sandbox, container and virtual-machine escapes, and any AI-evaluation incidents in which a system exploited its test environment, from primary sources rather than memory, and report them with dates.

## Track B — Who or What Can Be Contained, and the Criteria

**Questions**

- What classes of entity exist: embodied, distributed, informational or AI, temporal, ontological, negation-tier, bestowed vessels, holders of positional authority?
- For each class, what does "contained" mean? Is a prisoner who produces perfect copies contained, multiplied, or escaped?
- Which entities cannot be contained at all, and what does the institution do about them? The Unmaker (Entry 46) is explicitly not understood and must not be treated as a known prisoner type.
- Containment criteria and sentencing criteria are different questions. Containment asks what is necessary. Sentencing asks what is deserved or required, for how long, and who reviews it.
- What authority admits, what authority sentences, and what authority reviews? They need not be the same.
- What is the purpose of custody: punishment, protection, quarantine, research, or different purposes by sector?

**Real-world analogs:** civil commitment, public-health quarantine law, preventive detention, the treatment of prisoners of war, international criminal tribunals, and the UN Nelson Mandela Rules.

## Track C — Identity and Privileged Access

**Questions**

- What counts as the same individual after copying, transformation, restoration or possession?
- How is a staff member authenticated when biometrics are meaningless?
- How is coercion of a genuine staff member handled?
- How does recognition-based authority under the Invocation Framework coexist with institutional checks? Entry 17 already flags this tension, and I will keep it visible.
- Which peer proposals hold up? In particular I will test Gemini's tri-factor model and rotating session keys, and note where they rest on unresolved canon (for example, the purpose of the Nail Prototypes is still open).

**Real-world analogs:** NIST zero trust (SP 800-207) and security controls (SP 800-53), separation of duties, two-person integrity rules in nuclear weapons handling, break-glass emergency access, and insider-threat research.

## Track D — Control Systems, Monitoring and the Infinite Interior

**Questions**

- How does a finite system account for an infinite space? What is the bounded management surface, and what is it blind to?
- What happens when a flood of false RED alerts hides a real breach?
- How do confinement systems and life-support systems fail independently, so that a lock-down does not trap an injured staff member?
- What does a color-only alert assume about the staff who must read it?

**Real-world analogs:** alarm-flood failures in industrial accidents, industrial control system safety guidance, Perrow's normal-accident theory, and the layered-defense ("Swiss cheese") model of failure.

## Track E — Wardens, Governance and Management

**Questions**

- Is "warden" a single office or a family of roles?
- What prevents any single office, or the Spider Caste, from holding detection, pursuit, adjudication, punishment and evidence at once?
- Who audits the auditors, and what keeps records independent?
- What does a Sovereign Labyrinth, a treaty-governed Labyrinth and a distributed multi-civilization custodianship each cost and risk?
- Can the Labyrinth itself be sentenced, quarantined or replaced?

**Real-world analogs:** high-reliability organizations, independent prison inspection and oversight bodies, and documented prison-governance failures (cases to be selected and verified).

## Track F — Staffing: Extraterrestrial, AI and Other

**Questions**

- Why extraterrestrial at all? The strongest in-universe reason is probably independence from the prisoners' civilizations, and that needs testing.
- Does the design require extraterrestrial-only staffing, or is that a population choice? This is the Architect's call, so I will present the trade-offs of each.
- What is the trust model for each category: biological, synthetic, AI, collective, energy-based, Spider Caste, human?
- Can staff be captured, bribed, replaced or turned, and what then?
- Which AI roles are advisory, and which, if any, are persons with rights?
- Cautions from the peer reviews: the Greys and Puritans entries are underdeveloped, 1AI and -1AI are explicitly not routine staff, and loyalty or immunity claims about the Spider Caste are inference unless the Architect says otherwise.

**Real-world analogs:** published AI-control and oversight research, insider-threat case studies, and corrections-staffing research.

## Track G — Staff Living Area and Community

**Questions**

- Is the living area a settlement or an annex? Can staff end an assignment and leave?
- How does it stay causally separate from the structure's time and physics?
- What does it need to support: privacy, rest, family or bonded groups, medical care, worship or contemplation, communication home, rotation and repatriation?
- How does contamination, whether biological, informational or psychological, cross the boundary between the containment floor and the residence?
- How are staff protected from identifying gradually with management or with prisoners?

**Real-world analogs:** NASA behavioral-health and habitat research, Antarctic winter-over studies, submarine patrols, the Mars-500 isolation study, Biosphere 2 crew conflict, and research on correctional-officer stress.

## Track H — Funding and Resourcing

**Questions**

- Who pays, in universe? Candidate sources are ONE Industries, Lewis Corp, OAL, Accords contributions and the currency systems under development. The Post-Scarcity Exchange Ledger is still undefined, so I will not rely on it.
- If the setting is post-scarcity, what is actually scarce: staff, attention, legitimacy, trust?
- Is funding an attack surface? A funder can capture an institution.
- Is the claim that a prisoner's own escape attempts power the sentence workable, or is it a perpetual-motion trap?
- What happens to the institution if funding stops?

**Real-world analogs:** supermax and long-term prison costs, long-lived nuclear-waste repository funding and governance, biosafety-level-4 facility operation, and long-duration space-station budgets.

## Track I — Testing and Assurance

**Questions**

- How do you test a prison that is supposed to be inescapable? Likely answer: test everything except inescapability, and test the people.
- What success measures should replace "zero escapes"?
- How are tabletop exercises, pre-mortems and game days run for fictional systems?
- Who tests the testers, and how is independent verification kept independent?

**Real-world analogs:** red-team and force-on-force exercises, chaos engineering, independent verification and validation, and blameless post-incident review.

## Track J — Containment Failure Taxonomy

I will build a failure catalogue that separates at least these cases: unauthorized escape, authorized release, wrongful admission, substitution or replication, insider compromise, command capture, systemic and common-cause failure, governance failure, staff-welfare collapse, and slow loss of legitimacy. A key test is failures that occur while containment works perfectly.

## Track K — Environments and Domains

The Architect's note asks for environments and domains. I read "environments" as the types of space in which containment can occur (physical, informational and simulated, dimensional, temporal, ontological). I am not sure whether "domains" means the OAL domain-stewardship structure, the EAMA management domains, or something else. **[Open Question]** I will not assume, and I will ask before executing this track.
