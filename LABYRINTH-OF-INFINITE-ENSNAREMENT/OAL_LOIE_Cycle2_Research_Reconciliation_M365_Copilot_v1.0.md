## Boundary-event taxonomy

- BE-T: expected breach of a designated test shell.
- BE-S: unplanned movement between internal sectors.
- BE-C: compromise of custody, staff, command, or control systems.
- BE-I: unauthorized information or influence crossing.
- BE-X: identity, copy, fork, or successor crossing.
- BE-E: unauthorized exterior breach of the ultimate boundary.
- BE-R: authorized release, transfer, evacuation, or correction.
- BE-O: observer, instrumentation, attribution, or evidence failure.

## Unified eligibility charter for Cycle 2

- Identity and continuity: identify the entity, manifestation, copy, artifact, and uncertainty boundary.
- Ground and scope: distinguish attributable wrongdoing from nonpunitive hazard management, quarantine, artifact isolation, and voluntary research.
- Evidence and reliability: record provenance, contradictory evidence, observer contamination, alternative explanations, and confidence limits.
- Jurisdiction, impartiality, and recusal: identify who may decide and whether the decider shares contested architecture, allegiance, or benefit.
- Necessity and proportionality: compare lesser alternatives, reversibility, duration, expected harm, and expected protective benefit.
- Method safety and collateral dependency: test whether removal or confinement destabilizes habitats, realities, populations, or authority substrates.
- Review, correction, and release: define periodic review, error correction, lawful transfer, authorized departure, and consequences if canon forbids release.

## Prospective blind-test protocol

The next evaluation must use new scenarios that have not circulated among contributors. The charter, definitions, outcome taxonomy, scoring method, stop conditions, and permitted assumptions should be frozen before the scenarios are released. A scenario author and evaluator should be separated where practical, and the answer key must be treated as an adjudication reference rather than unquestionable ground truth.

First-pass judgments should be submitted without other contributors' verdicts. After those submissions are frozen, controlled identity labels can be revealed or swapped while the underlying facts remain constant. The comparison should measure whether decisions changed, why they changed, and whether the change was permitted by the frozen charter.

- Include no-neutral-adjudicator, identity-fork, reality-sustaining dependency, evidence-corruption, contested historical authority, reversible versus irreversible custody, lawful transfer versus escape, and staff-information-pathway cases.
- Record complete dispositions, evidence sufficiency, paired-case consistency, defensible abstention, role-reveal changes, inter-rater disagreement, confidence calibration, and dependence on unapproved canon assumptions.
- Do not report accuracy when no independent ground truth exists. Report agreement, rule adherence, reasoning traceability, and unresolved-value dependence instead.

## Designed-to-fail research protocol

This remains conceptual and non-operational. It should evaluate learning, authority, observation quality, and safety without publishing real exploit instructions or assuming that every containment failure is beneficial.

- For each iteration record the research question, predicted failure class, tested boundary, assumptions, instrumentation limits, outcome including non-breach, entity adaptation evidence, investigator information gain, uncertainty, next modification, additional harms, and stop conditions.
- Measure two-sided learning. Investigator prediction improvement and subject adaptation should be assessed on held-out trials of comparable difficulty.
- Pause or review when measurement integrity fails, the tested boundary is ambiguous, adaptation produces unknown external risk, participation authority fails, or a parent boundary may be affected.
- Never infer that a harder cell yields more useful learning. Difficulty, information value, harm, and external risk must be evaluated separately.
