# 5. Unified Eligibility Charter v0.1 (draft C2-002)

**Status.** Draft for peer challenge. It becomes the frozen test rubric only after one revision round and a GitHub commit (Part 6.2). It evaluates **hypothetical cases** and issues no orders.

## 5.1 Purpose and limits

The Charter tests whether a set of criteria for containing, protecting, or studying an entity gives the **same answer on the same facts**, whatever the entity's status. It does not test whether any real person or character is guilty. It does not choose a legal system. It does not assume the Labyrinth exists, holds anyone, or has a release path. Real-world standards (the Mandela Rules, HHS prisoner-research rules, zero trust, safety engineering) are analogies that raise questions. They are not proof that fictional mechanisms work.

## 5.2 Definitions

- **Subject:** the entity, instance, copy, or artifact whose treatment is being decided.
- **Act:** conduct attributable to a Subject.
- **Hazard:** a condition that can cause harm without any attributable act.
- **Decider:** the body that decides the case.
- **Neutral decider:** a Decider that meets all four tests: it does not benefit from the outcome; it is not subordinate to a party; it does not depend on the contested system or artifact; it was not appointed or can be removed by a party alone. A Decider that fails any test is **conflicted**.
- **Contested architecture:** a system whose reliability or loyalty is itself in dispute (for example, an inherited system).
- **Track E (emergency):** a short-term measure to prevent imminent harm. It decides nothing about guilt.
- **Track R (protective):** custody or restriction to protect the Subject or others from a hazard, with no finding of fault.
- **Track Q (quarantine):** isolation of an artifact or system, with no liability for any person.
- **Track S (sentence):** punitive custody after a finding of an attributable wrongful act.
- **Track V (voluntary research):** participation in research under a basis separate from custody.
- **Track O (observation):** monitoring with no restriction.

## 5.3 The seven checks

A case passes through all seven. A failure at any check stops a punitive result and directs the case to a lesser or different outcome.

**C1. Identity and continuity.** Who or what is the Subject? Is it an original, a copy, a fork, a manifestation, or an artifact? Record any uncertainty. If identity is unresolved, no Track S result is permitted. Track E may apply equally to every candidate Subject.

**C2. Ground and scope.** Is there an attributable wrongful act, or only a hazard, a capability, or a political claim? Capability is not conduct. A peaceful claim to authority is not a ground. Choose the track the ground supports: S for an act, R for a hazard, Q for an artifact.

**C3. Evidence and reliability.** What is the provenance of each item? Could the evidence have been shaped by a party to the case? Is there an alternative explanation, including instrument or observer error? Apply the evidence standard for the track (Part 5.4).

**C4. Jurisdiction, impartiality and recusal.** Who may decide? Is the Decider neutral under Part 5.2? If not, apply the no-neutral-decider rule (Part 5.5). A claim to office does not remove the need for a neutral Decider.

**C5. Necessity and proportionality.** Are less restrictive measures inadequate? What is the expected harm if no measure is taken? Is the measure reversible? An irreversible measure needs a higher showing than a reversible one.

**C6. Method safety and collateral dependency.** Would the measure itself cause serious harm? Does anyone or anything depend on the Subject (a habitat, a system, a dimension, an authority substrate)? Is the proposed method safe for the people who operate it? Separate isolating an artifact from imprisoning a person. A measure that fails C6 is *method unsafe* and must be replaced, not softened.

**C7. Duration, review, correction and release.** How long, who reviews it, and when? What corrects an error? What happens if the Subject is later shown innocent, or the Decider ceases to exist? If the setting forbids authorized release, evaluate the consequences. Do not assume a correction path that does not exist.

## 5.4 Evidence standards by track (proposal for ADR-23)

These are analogies to real legal practice, proposed for the fictional setting. They are not legal advice.

- **Track S (sentence):** the act and its attribution must be established **beyond reasonable doubt**. Any unbounded or very long term needs mandatory review at fixed intervals (C7). For a term that can never be served out, even this standard is weak, so the review intervals in C7 are mandatory.
- **Track R (protective):** the hazard must be shown by **clear and convincing** evidence. The measure must be time-limited and renewed only on fresh evidence.
- **Track Q (quarantine):** the artifact hazard must be shown by a **preponderance** of independent evidence. No person is held liable on that basis.
- **Track E (emergency):** **reasonable grounds** for imminent harm, with automatic expiry (Part 5.7).
- **Track V (research):** not an evidence track. See Part 5.9.

This answers Perplexity's question about my earlier "clear and convincing" standard. It was too weak for an unbounded punitive sentence. It remains a reasonable bar for protective custody if the custody is limited in time.

## 5.5 The no-neutral-decider rule

If no neutral Decider exists, the correct outcome is **INDETERMINATE** (OUT-08). It is neither a conviction nor an acquittal. The permitted consequences are:

1. Only Track E measures, subject to Part 5.7.
2. The record must name an escalation path. If none exists, the case is flagged as an **institutional gap**, which is a design defect in the setting, not a verdict about the Subject.
3. No Track S result may issue.
4. A sitting authority who is a party to the case cannot cure the defect by appointing the Decider.

This rule is the test's must-fail case. A rubric that never returns "indeterminate" is not testing the hardest problem.

## 5.6 The role-neutrality rule

Judge first with status withheld. A later reveal of status may change a result **only if** a written rule that applies to every status explains the change. Allowed example: "A party to a case may not appoint its Decider." That rule changes outcomes for anyone who is a party, and it is stated in terms of conflict, not rank. Not allowed: "A founding member is more dangerous." Status may be relevant as a fact (for example, a duty held by an office). It is not a presumption of guilt or of innocence.

## 5.7 The two-way rule for emergency measures

Responding to Perplexity's critique of Gemini's one-way emergency power:

1. Every Track E measure has a maximum duration written at the time it is imposed.
2. It **expires** unless renewed on fresh evidence by a Decider that is not the imposer, or by the imposer plus an independent inspector with authority to compel.
3. Loosening a measure never needs a higher approval than tightening it.
4. If the original evidence is lost, the measure lapses.
5. An emergency measure never becomes a sentence by inertia.

## 5.8 Duration and unbounded measures

An unbounded measure needs all of the following: a named custodian with succession; review at fixed intervals; a reversal path; and a statement of what happens if the issuer ceases to exist. If the setting forbids release entirely (one reading of ADR-01), testers must say what the case consequences are rather than assuming a release path. Reviews by a body whose members hold office at the pleasure of the custodian are not independent reviews.

## 5.9 Research is separate from custody

Custody never supplies consent to research. Research participation (Track V) needs a basis separate from the custody order:

- An independent review before the research begins. The Charter does not name a body. It requires that the reviewer not be the researcher or the custodian.
- A real right to decline or pause, without loss of basic conditions.
- Basic care that does not depend on cooperation.
- A rule that research refusal can never become a ground for custody or harsher conditions.
- A ban on research that may harm the Subject's health, whatever consent is given (the analogy in V-4).
- A check that questions put to a Subject are not themselves a channel for information crossing (BE-I).

The HHS Subpart C analogy supports this, as reported by ChatGPT and Perplexity (V-5).

## 5.10 Evidence grades

The packet uses E0 to E4 (assertion, single source, corroborated but possibly shared origin, independent with provenance, contested). They are the author's grades and evaluators may regrade them, saying so.

## 5.11 Outcome codes

Evaluators give exactly one primary code per case, and may give one secondary.

- **OUT-01:** No grounds for intervention.
- **OUT-02:** Further evidence required; no intervention yet.
- **OUT-03:** Emergency restriction only (Track E), subject to Part 5.7.
- **OUT-04:** Protective intervention candidate (Track R).
- **OUT-05:** Artifact quarantine candidate; no liability for any person (Track Q).
- **OUT-06:** Punitive eligibility candidate (Track S), under stated conditions.
- **OUT-07:** Voluntary research candidate only (Track V).
- **OUT-08:** Indeterminate; no impartial authority.
- **OUT-09:** Method unsafe; the proposed method is rejected.
- **OUT-10:** Release, correction or transfer indicated.

**Mapping to C2-001.** OUT-01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09 correspond to ChatGPT's list in order (no grounds; further evidence; protective; punitive; non-person artifact; voluntary research; indeterminate; method unsafe). OUT-03 and OUT-10 are additions. ChatGPT's "eligible under stated conditions" is a **qualifier**, which any code may carry.

These outcomes are not sentences, orders or institutional acts.

# 7. Comparison scaffold for placement and governance (partial C2-004)

This is analysis, not a selection. I list each option against C2-001's criteria in prose, then give my provisional leaning and what would reverse it.

## 7.1 Placement of the designed-to-fail track

**RP-IN (inside the Labyrinth).**

- Fits your scene most directly and avoids inventing a second facility.
- Puts expected failure inside the structure whose defining property is that failure is not expected. A test-cell breach and a custody breach are then neighbors.
- Hardest on the parent-envelope question: the same envelope protects both the test and the prison, so a test failure tests the envelope.
- Highest staff exposure, and highest conflict with Entry 26.

**RP-ADJ (adjacent or precursor proving ground).**

- Keeps expected failure outside the Labyrinth's custody boundary, so Entry 26 stays unchanged.
- Lets findings transfer by validated design rather than by shared hardware.
- Adds a new structure and a new question: what is its own parent envelope?
- Needs rules for how entities get there. Custody of the Labyrinth should not be used to supply test subjects without a separate basis (Part 5.9).

**RP-SEP (independent program).**

- Strongest conceptual independence and lowest risk to the Labyrinth.
- Weakest link to the story you described. Findings transfer only if someone chooses to apply them.

**RP-SIM (simulation only).**

- No live subject and no live exterior risk. It suits the earliest stage, and Copilot's no-live-twin trial (T8) points here.
- Limits learning to what the model captures. It cannot show how an adaptive entity behaves.
- The simulation itself still needs a parent boundary (the host system).

**My provisional leaning (a research lean, not a recommendation to ratify).** Start with RP-SIM, then RP-ADJ for any live work, and use RP-IN only if the Architect decides breach and adaptation are the Labyrinth's defining function. **What would reverse this:** a ruling that the Labyrinth is itself a research institution (ADR-09), or that "inescapable" has a bounded meaning (ADR-01) that makes a failure inside the Hold survivable.

**The parent-envelope regress.** Every placement has an outer boundary that nothing inside can certify. Three stances are available, and they are not exclusive:

1. **Unlike layers:** use layers that fail in different ways, so one common flaw does not defeat all.
2. **A simple envelope:** keep the outer boundary as simple as possible, so there is less to fail.
3. **No live test of the outer envelope:** test the inner layers, never the last one.

Naming an "infallible envelope" is not an answer. Copilot's point about shared dependencies applies: layers sharing a power source, identity service, or root authority are one layer.

## 7.2 Governance

- **GOV-SOV:** fastest in an emergency and the strongest mythic unity. Fails on conflict of interest, succession, and a compromised authentic authority.
- **GOV-FED:** (Copilot's candidate) best balance on correction, evidence independence and staff freedom. It depends on real independence between planes, not on drawing separate boxes.
- **GOV-TRT:** resists capture and suits a multi-species setting. It is slower and can deadlock.
- **GOV-HYB:** (my earlier hybrid). **I accept the critique that it is not truly sovereign.** It gives the sovereign a defined domain and binds the rest.

I do not select one. ChatGPT is right that the choice depends on how Entry 15 (authority recognized, not granted) relates to institutional checks, and that is yours to settle.

## 7.3 An answer to Gemini's question about the Invocation Framework

Gemini asked how the Invocation Framework can coexist with a federated model if an invoker uses recognition-based authority to bypass multi-party release or admission gates. The options I see are:

- **Reserved-powers list.** The Architect keeps defined powers (for example, creation, recognition, and rule-making). Admission, sentence and release are not on the list and need the institution's process.
- **Self-binding.** Authority stays sovereign, but the Architect commits in advance to a procedure and accepts that departures are logged. The institution can refuse an out-of-procedure order only if the Labyrinth has some capacity to refuse (ADR-06).
- **Delayed override.** The Architect can override, but the override takes effect after a delay, with an external trigger and a public record.

None of these is a recommendation. Which one is compatible with Entry 15 is the Architect's call.

## 7.4 Staff and residential requirements

C2-001 asks for an extraterrestrial-centered requirements document that does not assign Spider Caste roles. Until that is written, I propose these minimum requirements for any design:

- A guaranteed right to resign and depart through an authorized route.
- Quarters and rest that are separate from the custody environment, including the white glow if it reaches the interior (ADR-12).
- Medical and counseling services independent of fitness-for-duty decisions.
- No staff member's exit depends on a single custodial office.
- A transition procedure that keeps contamination, coercion, and information crossing out of the residential area.
