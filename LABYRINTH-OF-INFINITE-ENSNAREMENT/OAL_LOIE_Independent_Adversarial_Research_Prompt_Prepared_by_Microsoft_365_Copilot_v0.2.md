This v0.2 prompt supersedes Copilot v0.1 for future execution while preserving v0.1 as a historical artifact. ## 3. Research Method

For every claim: identify provenance; distinguish canon, Architect direction, empirical evidence, analogy, proposal, inference, and unknown; use current authoritative or primary sources; find contrary evidence; state limitations; define a falsification test; and preserve unresolved decisions.

The research must not aim to prove the Labyrinth correct. It must be able to conclude that a preferred mechanism, sentence, staffing model, or test is unsupported, unsafe, illegitimate, or untestable.

## 4. Central Research Question

How should an infinite, multispecies, multi-entity containment institution be designed when selected experimental containers are expected to fail and generate learning, while the institution must still preserve legitimate authority, scientific validity, staff safety, ethical limits, continuity, and the canon of an inescapable ultimate prison?

## 5. Required Research Domains

### 5.1 Containable Subjects and Hazards

Develop a taxonomy covering biological persons, extraterrestrial entities, synthetic life, robots, AI models and agents, distributed and collective minds, copies and forks, transferred consciousness, energy or informational entities, artifacts, processes, environments, memetic hazards, and explicitly fictional temporal, dimensional, ontological, and narrative classes.

For every class define: identity, continuity, containment meaning, escape meaning, observation limits, care needs, staff compatibility, false-positive harm, and conditions making live testing inappropriate.

### 5.2 Admission, Containment, and Sentencing

Separate punitive sentencing, protective containment, quarantine, emergency custody, voluntary research participation, involuntary research observation, and custody of non-person hazards. Research authority, jurisdiction, evidence, necessity, proportionality, least-restrictive alternatives, duration, review, appeal, correction, transfer, release, care, and representation.

Capability is not guilt. Technical containability is not sentencing legitimacy.

### 5.3 Adaptive Containment Through Expected Failure

Model a series of containers intentionally expected to fail. Researchers and the entity understand the study. Each breach is observed, causally reconstructed, and used to design a different next test. The response must not default to harsher or disproportionately difficult confinement.

Require at least six iterations. One must become simpler because complexity caused failure. One must fail through staff, management, or trust-handoff error. One apparent escape must be a measurement or identity error. One must show that the entity learned from prior trials. Include stop conditions and a no-live-test alternative.

For every iteration record: hypothesis, boundary, instrumentation, known weakness, breach class, evidence integrity, researcher learning, entity learning, minimal modification, new risk, ethics review, and stop/continue decision.

### 5.4 AI Sandboxes and Trust Boundaries

Research whether sandboxing is the dominant AI-agent containment approach or one layer among VMs, microVMs, containers, capability restrictions, least privilege, segmentation, tool mediation, offline evaluation, and external monitoring.

Separate genuine boundary escape, container breakout, control-plane weakness, shared-kernel weakness, unsafe permissions, egress, credential exposure, prompt injection, persistence, trust-handoff failure, and unsafe external consumption of sandbox-produced files. Abstract exploit details into defensive failure classes.

### 5.5 Identity and Privileged Access

Research zero trust, privileged-access management, separation of duties, just-in-time authority, revocation, coercion, insider threats, audit independence, and conflicts between recognized creator authority and delegated governance.

Test copies, restoration, body exchange, possession, shared minds, temporal displacement, memory alteration, and credentials inherited after death or restoration. Separate privileges for observation, research, maintenance, admission, adjudication, sentencing, transfer, release, life support, and emergency response.

### 5.6 Controls, Wardens, and Management

Compare centralized, distributed, federated, and hybrid control. Separate containment, identity, authority, habitat/life safety, evidence, monitoring, research generation, sentencing records, and independent review. Test common-mode failure, alarm flooding, degraded operation, succession, compromised authentic users, unavailable leadership, and offline fallback.

Preserve WHITE and RED. Any additional state is a proposal.

### 5.7 Extraterrestrial, AI, Spider Caste, Human, and Other Staff

Compare extraterrestrial-only, extraterrestrial-majority, and mixed staffing. Treat origin, embodiment, personhood, competence, consent, environmental compatibility, independence, accountability, and rights as separate axes. Do not assign the Spider Caste a role, consciousness, loyalty, or immunity by inference. Distinguish AI tools, advisers, embodied agents, and possible AI persons.

### 5.8 Staff Settlement

Research atmosphere, pressure, gravity, radiation, light, noise, nutrition, waste, medicine, rest, privacy, recreation, culture, communication home, families or bonded groups, disability access, quarantine, emergency refuge, leave, resignation, asylum, repatriation, and freedom to depart through authorized procedures. Staff must not become another captive population.

### 5.9 Environments and Domains

### 5.10 Failure, Recovery, and Testing

Create failure classes for boundary breach, partial or undetected breach, authorized movement misclassification, copy or identity escape, information/influence escape, staff compromise, sensor deception, common-mode failure, provenance corruption, AI drift, life-support failure, habitat contamination, wrongful admission, jurisdiction failure, sentence corruption, cascade, and institutional failure despite physical containment.

Metrics must include learning quality, detection, consequence containment, staff safety, record integrity, reversibility, correction, auditability, care continuity, and new vulnerabilities, not only zero escapes.

### 5.11 Funding

### 5.12 Ethics and Research Governance

Ask how finite administrators manage unbounded space, define a section, locate occupants, prove absence of breach, move staff, retrieve entities, preserve addresses, and avoid substituting the word infinite for an operating model.

## 6. Foundational Architects as a Test Class - Architect Containment Inquiry

Do not presume a Foundational Architect is imprisoned, guilty, containable, or exempt. Use this category to test whether criteria apply consistently to foundational, creative, administrative, or reality-shaping authority.

Ask separately:

1. **Can an architect be contained?** A capability, identity, access, topology, and systems question.
2. **Should an architect be contained?** An evidence, authority, necessity, proportionality, legitimacy, rights, and sentencing question.

Extraordinary capability, authorship, ownership, knowledge, or status does not by itself prove culpability. Architect status must not automatically create immunity if neutral criteria are met.

### 6.1 Architect Taxonomy

Distinguish original Foundational Architects; successor architects; administrative architects; former or revoked architects; architects whose work retains residual intent; distributed, copied, restored, collective, or time-displaced architects; architects who are custodians, judges, owners, witnesses, victims, or beneficiaries; architects recognized by legacy systems; and title-only architects versus entities with actual root-level power.

### 6.2 Candidate Criteria to Test

Adversarially evaluate whether containment could be considered when independently reviewable evidence supports ongoing or imminent catastrophic harm; repeated misuse of foundational authority; unauthorized alteration or coercive control of inhabited realities; corruption of identity, evidence, sentencing, or governance; violent or deceptive reclamation through residual intent or legacy access; creation of continuing uncontrollable harm; circumvention of oversight or succession; privileged compromise of containment; or a demonstrated failure of less restrictive alternatives.

These are hypotheses, not canon. Require jurisdiction, evidence standards, causal connection, responsibility, necessity, proportionality, alternatives, duration, review, and correction.

### 6.3 Testability and Evaluator Problem

Research historical/provenance review, bounded simulations, counterfactuals, privilege-revocation exercises, independent residual-intent audits, non-self-incriminating behavioral evaluation, creator-backdoor red teams, and tests of less restrictive controls.

An architect may have authored the test, possess root access, be recognized as creator, alter evidence, or manipulate success criteria. Another office administering the same architecture does not make the test independent. State what cannot be tested reliably.

### 6.4 Required Architect Scenarios

Analyze at least six scenarios:

1. Catastrophic capability with no proven harmful act.
2. Credible allegation of continuing large-scale harm with disputed evidence.
3. A former architect using residual intent or legacy access to reclaim control.
4. Catastrophic past harm but low present risk.
5. An architect essential to maintaining reality or the Labyrinth.
6. A Labyrinth contributor who may exploit creator recognition but voluntarily submits to evaluation.

For each, separately assess investigation, temporary restriction, research observation, protective containment, punitive sentencing, alternatives, or no confinement. Do not force imprisonment.

### 6.5 Governance and Conflict of Interest

Test recusal, independent adjudication, treaty authority, distributed custodianship, temporary emergency power, evidence independence, appeal, whether one architect may contain another, whether the Labyrinth may refuse an architect's order, whether the Architect of Record can be evaluated under the same criteria, and who audits the authority declaring an architect containable.

### 6.6 Adaptive-Breach Connection

Use a Foundational Architect as one candidate for the designed-to-fail study without declaring that identity canon. Test whether valid learning is possible when the subject may have created the architecture, understands the researcher's design language, or selectively reveals escape methods to train the system toward a hidden objective.

Stop if the subject can manipulate the generator, observers, provenance, or parent boundary so thoroughly that causal learning is not trustworthy. Shift to simulation, external review, or non-performance.
