# 1. Purpose and Execution Gate

This prompt defines the independent research I will carry out on the Labyrinth of Infinite Ensnarement. The research is meant to help design its operating layer: containment, criteria, sentencing, identity and access, control, wardens, staffing, the staff settlement, funding, testing and failure.

It is adversarial by design. Every proposal must meet its strongest objection, and the research must be able to conclude that something the team likes does not hold up.

**Execution gate.** Preparing this document does not authorize the research. It will be circulated, revised on the Architect's request, and executed only when the Architect says so.

# 3. The Architect's Scenes, Restated Faithfully

I restate these first so the research does not drift from them.

## 3.1 The expected-breach series

A very hard-to-contain entity is placed in a container designed to eventually fail. When it breaches, researchers observe how. The environment then generates a new containment environment in response. The cycle repeats for each contained entity. Failure and breach are expected and foundational. The researchers do not simply make each response disproportionately harder. They change what they do based on the breach and study in detail how it happened. Both the entity and the researchers understand where they stand.

**What this is not.** It is not an unwitting intruder in a honeypot, an endless escalation of punishment, or proof of an unbeatable outer cage. Mutual understanding also does not by itself establish informed consent, consent to every method, or freedom to withdraw.

## 3.2 The Foundational Architect question

The Architect asks: should an architect be contained? When would an architect meet criteria for containment? Is that testable? Is a Foundational Architect an entity that might meet those criteria? What would someone have to do to meet them? The Architect wants these questions built into the research so that scenarios like this can be tested.

I read this as a request to use Foundational Architect containment as a test case for the criteria themselves, not as a request to decide whether the Founding Architects belong in the Labyrinth. Entry 6 leaves that relationship open, and I will not settle it.

# 4. Core Research Questions

**CRQ1.** Can containment be designed for expected failure, with breaches studied and used, while the Labyrinth itself stays inescapable?

**CRQ2.** Where does the expected-breach series sit relative to the Labyrinth, and what does each placement do to the inescapability canon?

**CRQ3.** What criteria, if any, would justify containing an Architect-tier entity, and can those criteria be tested for fairness, consistency and error?

CRQ1 and CRQ2 expose a tension that I think the team has not fully named. Entry 26 describes a prison that does not fail. The scene describes containers built to fail. Gemini proposes that the Labyrinth is inescapable precisely because its inner containers fail safely. That is a candidate resolution, not an established one, and I will test it alongside the alternatives rather than adopt it.

# 5. Hypotheses To Try To Falsify

- **H1.** Containment designed with breach as an expected event is safer than containment designed as fail-proof.
- **H2.** Studying each breach converges toward better containment rather than an arms race in which the entity learns faster than the researchers.
- **H3.** A breached test boundary can be reliably distinguished from escape into protected infrastructure or the outside.
- **H4.** Separate control planes for confinement, life support, identity, authority, records and oversight prevent a single failure from cascading.
- **H5.** A bounded management surface lets finite staff administer an infinite interior without blind spots that defeat the design.
- **H6.** A sentence with no end can remain legitimate if wrongful admission can be corrected.
- **H7.** Containment criteria can be written so that they give the same verdict when the roles in a scenario are swapped. Criteria that cannot do this are verdicts, not criteria.
- **H8.** Capability to escape, taken alone, never justifies punitive custody. At most it justifies protective containment, with review.

For each, I will state what would change my mind and report honestly if the evidence points the other way.

# 6. Research Tracks

## Track A — The Expected-Breach Series

**Placements to compare (all provisional):**

- An experimental sector inside the Labyrinth.
- A separate research facility that informs Labyrinth design, or precedes any sentence.
- A distinct containment-research concept with only thematic links.

I will ask whether an authorized research-sector breach counts as an incident under the existing RED rule. I will also keep these cases separate: a breached test cell, a sector breach, a custody breach, a breach of the exterior boundary, information exfiltration, escape of identity or a copy without escape of the body, and authorized release or transfer.

**The regress problem.** If the entity escapes almost everything, what justifies confidence in the outer envelope that is supposed to hold it? An independently protected experimental envelope is a candidate safeguard, not an established feature. I will investigate the regress directly. I will also include an eligibility decision that rejects live testing when bounded risk cannot be justified, with a simulation or no-test alternative.

**Three readings of "escape" to keep apart:**

- **Experimental containment:** nested test environments designed to fail safely inside a stronger boundary.
- **Operational adaptive containment:** a real incident in an inhabited sector prompts careful revision, and harm to staff counts as a first-class safety measure.
- **Deceptive or simulated escape:** the entity seems to leave but remains inside a parent environment. I will study when this is a valid test, when it corrupts learning, and what disclosure it needs.

**Rules for the study model.**

- Researchers change what they do in response to the breach. They make the smallest justified change, not the biggest available one. The sequence must not simply get stronger.
- At least six iterations, each testing a different hypothesis. At least one gets simpler because complexity caused the breach. At least one fails through a staff, management or trust-boundary problem rather than the entity overpowering the container. At least one apparent escape turns out to be a measurement, identity or definition error. At least one shows that the entity learned from earlier tests, which confounds the experiment. The series needs a stop condition for when further testing is no longer justified.
- For every iteration, record the hypothesis, the boundary assumptions, any vulnerabilities left in on purpose, the observation plan, the breach path and its class, what the researchers learned, what the entity probably learned, the minimal change, the new risks that change creates, and whether the next trial is still justified.

**Dual learning and deception.** The entity may feign failure, learn the experimenter, teach a false explanation, poison the telemetry, or escape through the observer or the environment generator rather than the test boundary. I will study sandbagging, Goodhart-style metric failure, specification gaming, repeated-trial dependence and observer effects. A pre-registered, immutable hypothesis record is a candidate control.

**Research is not a sentence.** Being studied is not the same as being punished. Admission to custody does not authorize experimentation. A very effective escaper is a valuable source of observations and is not necessarily a justified permanent prisoner.

**Analogies and where they break:** honeypots and honeynets (the intruder usually does not know), chaos engineering (it studies faults, not a strategic captive), red teaming, cyber ranges, adaptive control, assurance cases and AI-control research. For each I will say what it does not cover.

**On the sandbox question.** I will treat "sandbox is the go-to containment" as a question, not a premise. I will compare application sandboxes, containers, virtual machines, microVMs, capability restrictions, network isolation, monitoring and organizational controls. For each reported AI or agent escape I will identify the boundary tested, the privileges and weaknesses given, and whether the result generalizes. I will separate genuine boundary escape from misconfiguration, tool-permission failure, trust-handoff failure, prompt injection and evaluation-harness weakness. No claim that AI has escaped all containment will be accepted from a single report.

## Track B — Who Can Be Contained, and the Criteria

**Taxonomy.** Biological and extraterrestrial entities; humans; synthetic and engineered beings; software and malware; AI models, agents and forks; collective and distributed minds; shapeshifters; energy-based beings; memetic or informational hazards; temporal, dimensional and ontological entities (as fictional categories); bestowed vessels; holders of positional authority; and non-person hazards such as artifacts, processes and places.

For each class I will ask: what does containment mean, what counts as escape, what counts as the same individual afterward, and what evidence would show containment is working. I will also ask which classes are uncontainable under current canon, which need only protective quarantine, and which should never be admitted.

**Kinds of custody to separate:** punitive, preventive, protective, quarantine, emergency and temporary, research by consent, involuntary research, containment pending identity verification, and containment of non-person hazards. For each I will set out authority and jurisdiction, evidentiary threshold, identity certainty, necessity and proportionality, the least restrictive alternative, duration and review, appeal and correction, and care and representation.

**Intake decision tree.** Containment criteria (what is necessary) are separate from sentencing criteria (what is deserved, for how long, reviewed by whom). Capability is not guilt. I will build a proposed intake decision tree and note that human-rights instruments such as the Nelson Mandela Rules are ethical analogies, not binding law for imagined nonhuman beings.

## Track C — The Foundational Architect Containment Test (new)

**Why this is useful as a test.** [Canon, Entry 6] Nathan became Architect of Record through an active seizure. A prior coalition was eliminated, its technology absorbed, and the coalition contained rather than destroyed. [Canon, Entry 7] Positional Sovereignty holds that the Makers' relationship to light was positional, a status conferred by position and not an intrinsic property. [Canon, Entry 15] Authority is recognized, not granted. Together these mean the party that holds the Labyrinth's keys may also be the party that seized the position of the people held in it. A framework for containing Architects that cannot be applied to its own author is suspect. That is exactly why it makes a good test.

**Questions.**

- Should an Architect be contained at all? Is "Architect" a status, a role, a capability or a pattern of conduct? Under Positional Sovereignty, are criteria applied to the person or to the position?
- Which criterion family could justify it? (1) Conduct: specific harmful acts, proven to a stated standard. (2) Risk: credible, imminent danger with necessity and least-restrictive means. (3) Capability alone: by H8, rejected as grounds for punishment, tested as grounds for protective quarantine. (4) Legitimacy and jurisdiction: who is entitled to judge. (5) Status alone: tested and, I expect, rejected. (6) Residual intent: containment because absorbed architecture may carry active intent (FA-Q8), tested as a preventive ground, with the objection that it punishes what someone's technology might do.
- What would an Architect have to do? I will build a scenario battery (Appendix A) of hypothetical conduct, labeled as hypotheticals and not as claims about what the Founding Architects did.
- Who decides, when the Architect is a party? I will compare an independent review chamber, a treaty body, distributed custodianship, and recusal rules. Grok and Perplexity both flagged this.
- Can the Labyrinth hold an Architect-tier entity? [Canon] The structure absorbed the Architect's energy bolt. [Open Question] Whether it holds an entity whose authority is recognized rather than granted is unresolved, and the additive versus subtractive asymmetry in Entry 7 bears on it.

**How to test it.**

- **Pre-register the criteria** before applying them, so they cannot be tuned to reach a wanted answer.
- **Run the scenario battery** with roles swapped: a Founding Architect as subject, the current Architect as subject, a neutral third party as subject, and a spoofed or coerced Architect.
- **Apply the criteria blind and independently.** Each collaborating AI applies the same pre-registered criteria without seeing the others' verdicts. Disagreements are logged under OAL-AIDR, not smoothed over.
- **Measure** consistency across role swaps, sensitivity to evidence quality, outcomes under wrongful accusation, and whether the review process can reverse a wrong verdict.
- **Report the limit.** This tests internal consistency in a fictional model. It does not show the model is true.

**Possible findings, any of which is acceptable.** Architects meet the criteria in some scenarios and not others. No non-arbitrary criteria exist for the position. The criteria depend on a jurisdiction that canon has not established. The answer is a matter of authorship, not research, and belongs to the Architect.

**Constraint.** Entry 6 does not say whether the displaced coalition was guilty or innocent, and the brother is unnamed. I will not assume either. The research must be able to represent wrongful containment as one of the scenarios.

## Track J — Testing and Assurance

- How do you test a prison that is meant to be inescapable? Likely answer: test everything except inescapability, and test the people.

A count of zero events does not prove zero risk.
- Methods: tabletop exercises, pre-mortems, fault injection, independent validation, regression after every change, and a no-live-test alternative.

These are hypothetical conduct scenarios for testing criteria. They are not claims about what any Founding Architect did.

For each scenario the test records the criteria applied, the evidence standard, who decided, the verdict, the verdict with roles swapped, and whether a wrong verdict could be reversed.
