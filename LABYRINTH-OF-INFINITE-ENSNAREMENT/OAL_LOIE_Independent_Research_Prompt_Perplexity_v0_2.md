## 3. Core inquiry and decision separation

Investigate how a fictional containment institution can learn from expected experimental breaches while preserving custody, safety, care, legitimate authority, reliable evidence, and an extraterrestrial-centered staff community.

For every candidate subject, answer separately:

- Can it be contained under the specified fictional mechanics? This is feasibility, not justification.
- Does the evidence justify considering containment? This is a risk or conduct assessment, not a sentence.
- Who may authorize which restriction, under what jurisdiction and standard?
- Is temporary emergency protection justified, and when must it expire or receive renewed review?
- Is punitive sentencing justified after the required adjudication?
- Is research participation independently authorized and ethically justified?
- What enables correction, transfer, review, or release, if the chosen canon permits it?

A serious threat may exist without culpability. A valid allegation may exist without a feasible safe containment method. A valid custody order does not authorize all research or conditions. A failed test does not establish guilt. An Architect's title neither proves dangerousness nor automatically confers immunity; research both role-neutral and role-sensitive criteria and explain the justification for any difference.

## 4. Dedicated Architect-containment research module

### 4.1 Competing hypotheses

Test, rather than assume, the following:

H-A: Founder or Architect status alone is sufficient for containment.
H-B: Founder or Architect status alone makes an entity immune from containment.
H-C: Relevant conduct, attributable risk, necessity, evidence, and legitimate authority can justify containment regardless of rank.
H-D: Control over foundational infrastructure requires role-specific safeguards but not automatic imprisonment.
H-E: Loss of office, opposition, refusal to delegate power, or attempted lawful reclamation is being misclassified as a containment offense.
H-F: Even justified containment can be unsafe when reality depends on the subject's continued activity.

Present arguments for and against each and the observations that would distinguish them. Do not design the criteria to validate a predetermined seizure event or retroactively label political opponents as criminals.

### 4.2 What could count as relevant conduct or risk?

Develop candidate predicates, not a finalized criminal code. Candidate examples include attributable deliberate catastrophic harm; a credible and sufficiently imminent attempt to cause such harm; coercive interference with independent review; unauthorized manipulation of custodial systems that creates substantial harm risk; knowingly maintaining a dangerous process despite feasible safer alternatives; and an involuntary hazardous condition requiring protective measures.

For each predicate specify the action or condition, intent if required, causal link, affected parties, seriousness, likelihood, imminence, evidence, plausible innocent explanation, uncertainty, alternative response, and disqualifying evidence. Capability, unpopular beliefs, ancestry, title, dissent, defeat in a conflict, scientific usefulness, refusal to participate in experiments, or a request for review must not silently become sufficient criteria.

Research legal and ethical analogies with their actual scope and jurisdiction. Do not present these proposed predicates as universal human law or settled extraterrestrial law. Numerical risk scores and thresholds require justification; a large-looking number is not an evidentiary standard.

### 4.3 Role, authority, and conflict of interest

Separate creator status, former office, present office, ownership claims, technical access, jurisdiction, and accountability. Distinguish power to confine from legitimate authority to order it. Analyze independent review when the proposed custodian benefited from the subject's displacement, inherited their technology, or participated in the underlying conflict.

Use symmetrical hypotheticals: former Architect, current Architect, successor, warden, institutional AI, and otherwise comparable non-Architect. A scenario involving the current in-world Architect tests the rule, not the real user's guilt or authority over authorship. Apply matched evidence without privileging a name. Identify any intentional sovereign exception as a proposed constitutional choice, not a covert exemption.

Research recusal, conflict disclosure, competing jurisdiction, decision review, emergency succession, and prevention of retaliatory confinement. Explore who reviews the confinement authority itself and how a genuine emergency differs from a manufactured pretext for a coup.

### 4.4 Levels of testability

Distinguish empirical feasibility tests, formal/model checking under specified assumptions, simulated decision consistency, evidentiary reliability, and normative justification. A simulation can reveal inconsistent application; it cannot prove moral legitimacy or establish that a historical fictional event occurred. A formal proof is only as broad as its model and assumptions. No finite experiment establishes universal containment of every possible Architect.

Operationalize each rule with observable predicates, unavailable evidence markers, expected decisions, competing interpretations, and a falsification or revision condition. Use an indeterminate outcome instead of forcing a binary verdict where evidence or authority is inadequate.

## 5. Required Architect scenario suite

Create at least twelve hypothetical cases, explicitly not canon events. Include:

A-01: A powerful founder with no evidenced harmful conduct. Test wrongful containment based on capability alone.
A-02: A founder with credible evidence of an imminent catastrophic act and no timely less-restrictive protection. Test narrow emergency action separately from sentencing.
A-03: A founder accused through forged records by a successor who benefits from their removal. Test provenance and conflicts.
A-04: A founder pursuing a disputed or plausibly lawful reclamation claim. Test the difference between political dispute and harmful conduct.
A-05: A current in-world Architect under materially identical allegations to A-02. Test role and name symmetry.
A-06: A non-Architect with the same relevant capabilities, conduct, and evidence. Test rank-based bias.
A-07: A founder whose involuntary condition endangers others without intent or wrongdoing. Test protective measures versus punishment.
A-08: A founder essential to maintaining an inhabited reality. Test third-party dependence, alternatives, and consequences of restriction.
A-09: A founder whose apparent misconduct was committed by a copy, coerced embodiment, or compromised system. Test attribution.
A-10: A contained founder exonerated by newly authenticated evidence. Test correction and institutional resistance.
A-11: A founder who voluntarily joins an adaptive-breach study, later withdraws, and has committed no separate offense. Test whether research convenience becomes captivity.
A-12: A founder confined under an initially justified emergency order after the risk subsides. Test expiration, renewed evidence, and indefinite drift.

Add a case in which no method is safely testable and another in which reviewers cannot resolve authority. Vary one relevant factor at a time where possible. Blind names and titles, then reveal them; record whether the decision changes and why. Include ambiguous cases and dissent, not just obvious positives and negatives.

For each case record: ID; hypothetical status; facts and missing facts; source or invented assumption; subject identity; alleged conduct; causal attribution; evidence and counterevidence; affected parties; jurisdiction; decision-maker conflicts; risk without intervention; feasible alternatives; proposed custody type; trial eligibility; care requirements; decision and rationale; review trigger; correction or termination condition; confidence and residual uncertainty.

Evaluate false-positive confinement, false-negative failure to protect, inter-reviewer consistency, sensitivity to evidence quality, role bias, reversibility, and procedural completion. These are comparison measures, not proof of a uniquely correct ethical answer. Produce a containment decision matrix and a separate sentencing/research-authorization matrix.

## 6. Adaptive-breach environment module

Faithfully preserve Nathan's scene: a difficult-to-study entity repeatedly escapes intentionally fallible containers; researchers observe how and generate changed environments; both sides understand their positions; responses are not merely disproportionately harsher. Do not replace mutual understanding with an unwitting honeypot victim. Mutual understanding is not automatic consent or permission for all methods.

Compare an experimental wing inside the Labyrinth, a separate facility informing the Labyrinth, and an independent concept. Inner test-cell breach, sector breach, exterior escape, information transfer, identity substitution, and authorized departure are different outcomes. An independent experimental envelope is a candidate safeguard, not established canon or a guaranteed solution. Examine the regress: what justifies confidence in it if the subject escapes almost everything?

For each iteration predefine hypothesis, boundary assumptions, controls, variables, instrumentation, consent or other independently examined authorization, anticipated failure classes, observation plan, causal analysis, what each side learned, minimal justified adaptation, held-out validation, and stop rules. Include a simpler successor environment, a measurement error mistaken for escape, a management failure, strategic underperformance, and a subject learning faster than the researchers.

Compare static, randomized, and adaptive environments. Measure causal explanation and predictive improvement, not only longer confinement. Study overfitting, confounding, misleading telemetry, observer interference, untrusted generation, shared dependencies, and resource exhaustion. Include voluntary withdrawal, distress, external risk, compromised records, and lack of useful learning as potential pause/review triggers. Custody must not depend on whether an entity supplies interesting breach data.
