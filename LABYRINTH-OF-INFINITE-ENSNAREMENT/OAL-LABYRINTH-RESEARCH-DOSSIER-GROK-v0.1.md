## 4. Domain B — Adaptive Breach-Learning Containment

[Researched Finding] Automated Moving Target Defense and high-interaction honeynets deliberately present environments expected to be probed or breached so that attacker behavior can be observed and the attack surface changed. NIST and commercial implementations emphasize continuous change of memory layout, addresses, or topology to raise attacker cost and capture data. Success is not zero breaches but measurable learning and increased difficulty for the attacker.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="35" />

[Analogy] The Architect’s scene aligns more closely with measured, learning-oriented successive environments than with pure punishment escalators or pure deception honeypots. Mutual understanding is a distinguishing feature.

[Fictional Mechanism Proposal] Three hypotheses:  
1. Experimental containment inside a stronger outer boundary.  
2. Operational adaptive containment responding to real incidents with measured protocol/topology changes.  
3. Deceptive/simulated escape (entity believes it has left but remains inside parent environment).  

Per-iteration protocol elements recommended: baseline model, hypothesis, boundaries, independent witness, event log, causal reconstruction, stop/rollback criteria, distinction between learning and mere restriction increase. Address observer effects and entity adaptive deception.

[Adversarial note] Risk that the entity learns faster than researchers (arms-race outcome). Outer-envelope regress remains: confidence in any experimental boundary is undermined if the entity escapes almost everything. Eligibility gate: reject live testing when bounded risk cannot be justified.

Breach-and-learning matrix (conceptual sketch, 12 classes — not operational instructions):  
1. Seam via allowed monitoring channel. 2. Residual-intent forgery of privileged token. 3. Temporal asymmetry exploitation. 4. Energy-absorption nullification of staff countermeasure. 5. Identity continuity fork. 6. Staff coercion during shift transition. 7. Topology reconfiguration creating new path. 8. Alert flooding (RED DDoS). 9. Habitat contamination vector. 10. Record falsification. 11. Observer-effect behavioral change. 12. Subtractive/Unmaker-class retroactive unmaking attempt.

## 5. Domain C — Identity, Privileged Access, and Continuity

[Researched Finding] NIST SP 800-207 Zero Trust Architecture: no implicit trust based solely on network location or asset ownership; authentication and authorization are discrete and dynamic per session; protect resources rather than network segments. Seven tenets include per-session access, dynamic policy, continuous posture monitoring.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="20" />

[Analogy] Inside an infinite, energy-absorbing volume, ordinary spatial proximity or energy signatures are insufficient. Privileged access must be enforced by something the structure itself recognizes (topological privilege, potence-linked, or recognition-based) rather than copyable credentials.

[Fictional Mechanism Proposal] Separate observation, intervention, sentence modification, and release authorities. Residual Maker intent (FA-Q8) is an explicit forgery vector for any access system built from or near absorbed architecture. Continuity problems: division, copying, restoration, timeline alteration. Staff themselves can become permanently ensnared if their access path is invertible.

## 8. Domain F — Staff Composition, Extended-Stay Living Areas, Funding, and Testing

[Researched Finding] NASA Human Integration Design Handbook and behavioral-health risk records identify isolation, confinement, sleep disruption, reduced privacy, and distance from Earth as risks to cognitive performance, behavioral health, and long-term outcomes. Analog studies (Antarctic, CHAPEA-type habitats) show measurable effects over months. Supermax/SHU literature (Grassian, Haney) documents perceptual distortion, cognitive narrowing, and elevated self-harm risk under prolonged isolation.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="40" />

[Analogy] ET staff change the failure modes (different circadian, atmospheric, social, and sensory requirements) but do not automatically eliminate them. A finite, topologically insulated habitat with species-compatible conditions, private space, and authorized leave/return is required. Pure isolation of staff recreates documented risks.

[Fictional Mechanism Proposal] Extraterrestrial core workforce remains a key feature; exclusivity vs. mixed (AI, Omnicellular, other) is an open comparison. 1AI/-1AI excluded from routine roles. Funding models must avoid dependency on contained entities or residual Maker architecture. Testing regimes should treat failure as data and include staff ability to report without retaliation and to end assignments.
