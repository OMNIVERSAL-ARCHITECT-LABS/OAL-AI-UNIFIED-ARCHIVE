## B. The central new research problem: deliberate, measured failure

The Architect describes a scenario involving an entity that repeatedly breaches deliberately fallible containment environments. Researchers know the current environment may fail. The contained entity also understands the premise. Researchers observe each escape or boundary crossing, study **how** the breach occurred, then create another containment environment informed by the observed mechanism. They do not reflexively increase difficulty or impose indiscriminate escalation; they vary the environment in response to measured behavior, preserve experimental comparability, and aim to learn about the entity and the containment system. The scene itself may be provided separately; do not pretend to have reviewed footage or text not yet supplied.

Research the proposed **Adaptive Breach-Learning Containment Program (ABLC-P)** as a *working label only*, not a canon name. Compare at least three hypotheses:

1. **Experimental containment:** nested or sequential test environments are designed to fail safely inside a stronger research boundary; a breach is a measurable experiment outcome, not necessarily a breach of the Labyrinth's ultimate perimeter.
2. **Operational adaptive containment:** genuine incidents in inhabited sectors provoke cautious revisions to protocols, systems, and facility topology; impacts on staff and other occupants become first-class safety measures.
3. **Deceptive or simulated escape:** the entity appears to leave an environment but remains within a controlled parent environment; determine when this is a valid test, when it corrupts learning, and what consent or disclosure is required.

Do not equate these cases. Define the distinction between an **inner test-cell breach**, **sector breach**, **custody breach**, **exterior boundary breach**, **information exfiltration**, **identity/continuity escape**, and **authorized release/transfer**. A designed-to-fail cell must not imply that the outer facility's canonically established inescapability has been revoked. Explicitly evaluate the potential contradiction if the Architect intends actual escapes from the ultimate boundary.

### Research protocol for each containment iteration

Specify: baseline entity model and prior knowledge; objective and hypothesis; environment design and boundaries; allowed observations; instrumentation and independent witness; consent and ethical constraints; anticipated breach paths; event log and causal reconstruction; controls and counterfactuals; model update; design of the next environment; stop/rollback criteria; and what counts as learning versus merely increasing restriction. Preserve both successes and failures, including uncertainty. Avoid giving real-world actionable escape or exploitation instructions; use abstract, defensive, simulated test cases and non-operational diagrams.

**Key methodological challenge:** an adaptive entity may deliberately vary behavior, conceal capabilities, feign failure, exploit researcher expectations, or manipulate the dataset. The observer may also change the experiment, so changes in outcome cannot automatically be attributed to better containment. Research deception, Goodhart's law, specification gaming, overfitting, distribution shift, attacker adaptation, observer effects, and causal inference. Include an explicit pre-registration or immutable hypothesis-record proposal and controls for misleading telemetry.

Build a *breach-and-learning matrix* containing at least 12 conceptual trials with distinct hypotheses and failure classes, not simply twelve stronger walls. Include controls for researcher safety, deception, sensory blind spots, false positives, spillover, and discontinuation. Present a fictional scene treatment separately from any proposed real-world simulation architecture.

### D1. Mandatory distinctions and alternative hypotheses

Before developing a facility-specific answer, define clearly: (a) creator or designer of an architecture; (b) former or current holder of positional sovereignty; (c) Founding Architect as a particular in-universe historical classification; (d) technical capability to modify a system; (e) legally accountable actor; and (f) person, collective, artifact, or institution. These categories can overlap but must not be conflated. Do not treat titles as evidence of wrongdoing, powers as evidence of intent, or political defeat as judicial proof.

Test at least six competing positions: (H-A) an Architect may be contained under the same general standards applicable to comparable entities; (H-B) enhanced capabilities create special *safety precautions* but no reduced evidentiary rights; (H-C) architectural sovereignty creates jurisdictional obstacles that invalidate ordinary custodial claims; (H-D) a former Architect may pose a unique residual-access risk even without current malicious conduct; (H-E) confinement is unjustified if a less restrictive safe alternative remains viable; (H-F) a particular Founding Architect might satisfy objective criteria through proven conduct or demonstrated ongoing hazard—but the group as a whole is not automatically eligible. Include an explicit seventh null hypothesis: the evidence is insufficient to establish eligibility for containment at all.

### D2. Proposed testable eligibility framework (not canon)

Independently assess four sequential gates and document all missing information:

1. **Entity and jurisdiction gate.** Can the candidate be identified with defensible continuity across copies, embodiments, timelines, or distributed existence? Who claims legal or treaty jurisdiction? Is that claim legitimate, contested, or self-serving? What standards govern an actor who participated in creating the legal order?
2. **Substantive grounds gate.** Is there independently corroborated conduct, a sufficiently established ongoing hazard, or another defined legal basis? Distinguish past creation of dangerous technology from specific misuse; structural influence from harmful action; legitimate dissent from coercion or sabotage; capacity for catastrophic harm from actual culpability.
3. **Necessity and proportionality gate.** What harms are foreseeable, with what evidence and uncertainty? Can monitoring, revocation of access, disarmament, isolation of technologies, negotiated restrictions, supervised restoration, or other less-restrictive measures manage the risk? Can confinement prevent a harm without creating greater risk?
4. **Procedural and continuity gate.** Who investigates, adjudicates, authorizes custody, and independently reviews it? Can the decision be contested, corrected, renewed, revoked, or transferred? What happens if the Architect of Record or a Founding Architect is directly implicated in a disputed order? Can staff and auditors operate without depending on the contested architecture?

For each gate specify: measurable indicators; admissible evidence; evidence sources independent of the interested authority; standards for ambiguity; a **disconfirming observation**; minimum information needed before proceeding; and a finding of *eligible / not eligible / unresolvable on available evidence*. This is a conceptual decision framework for fictional scenarios, **not** a claim that law or empirical science can measure omniversal entities.
