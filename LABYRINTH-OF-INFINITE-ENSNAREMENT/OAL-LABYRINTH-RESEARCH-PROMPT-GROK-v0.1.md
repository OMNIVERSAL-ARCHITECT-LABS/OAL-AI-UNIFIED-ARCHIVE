**Foundational Designed-to-Fail Scene**

## 3. Core Research Domains and Required Questions

### 3.1 Topology, Environments, and Infinite Interior
- What operational rules allow a finite cubic exterior to contain an infinite interior while permitting finite staff to locate, monitor, and respond?  
- Static Hilbert-type volume vs. dynamically generated maze vs. actively reconfiguring non-orientable structure: failure modes of each.  
- Finite “management surface” or insulated staff habitat vs. full exposure to infinity.  
- Temporal asymmetry: inmate post-temporal or orthogonal time vs. staff sequential time. How is staff habitat insulated?  
- Energy absorption: double-edged implications for inmate force, staff actions, and power systems.  

### 3.2 Identity, Privileged Access, and Continuity
- Authentication when spatial proximity and ordinary energy signatures are insufficient or absorbable.  
- Residual Maker intent (FA-Q8) as forgery or recognition vector.  
- Continuity across division, copying, restoration, timeline alteration, or embodiment change.  
- Separation of observation, intervention, sentence modification, and release authorities.  
- Can staff themselves become permanently ensnared?  

### 3.3 Control Systems, Wardens, and Management
- Scalability of WHITE/RED sectional alerts in infinite volume.  
- Independence of containment, habitat safety, identity/access, custody records, monitoring, and audit planes.  
- Spider Caste: biological, technological, hybrid, or other? Role relative to ET staff and AI.  
- Who escalates or overrides? What survives residual intent or long-term exposure?  

### 3.4 Sentence, Custody, Review, and Error Correction
- Operational meaning of a sentence that outlasts the end of time.  
- Distinction between punitive, protective, quarantine, and research-containment purposes.  
- Mechanisms (if any) for correcting wrongful admission without creating an unauthorized-escape vector.  
- Authority when the Architect is party to the underlying dispute or unavailable.  

### 3.5 Staff Composition and Extended-Stay Living Areas
- Extraterrestrial staff as key feature: advantages and monoculture/blind-spot risks.  
- Eligibility of AI (including Paragon Cube-linked systems), Omnicellular, bestowed entities, or others. Note 1AI/-1AI are not ordinary bots.  
- Psychological and physiological effects of extended proximity to infinite, time-orthogonal, energy-absorbing environments (drawing on isolation literature as analogy only).  
- Finite, topologically insulated staff habitat requirements: atmosphere, gravity, circadian, social, privacy, medical, leave/return.  
- Test: can staff end an assignment and leave through authorized procedures?  

### 3.6 Designed-to-Fail Research Environments
- Architecture of successive, expected-to-fail containers that generate new environments upon observed breach.  
- Study protocol: detailed post-breach analysis shared in mutual understanding between researchers and entity.  
- Risks: entity learning faster than researchers; contamination of primary Labyrinth; staff radicalization or ontological erosion; creation of an uncontained “escape curriculum.”  
- Funding and institutional sponsorship models for such research tracks.  
- Testing methodologies that treat failure as data rather than defect.  

### 3.7 Funding, Testing, and Institutional Continuity
- In-universe funding or resource models that do not create dependency on the contained entities or residual Maker architecture.  
- Test regimes for containment, access control, staff welfare, and designed-failure tracks.  
- Succession and continuity when senior authorities are unreachable or compromised.
