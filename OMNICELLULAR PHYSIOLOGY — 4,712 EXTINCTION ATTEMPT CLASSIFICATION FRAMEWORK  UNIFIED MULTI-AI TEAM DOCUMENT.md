## PART I — MASTER DEFINITIONS: WHAT COUNTS AS AN EXTINCTION ATTEMPT

*Synthesized from: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity | Cross-validated against scientific literature*

### 1.1 The Core Definition

Six AI systems were asked independently to define an extinction attempt. Their definitions converge on the same core standard, and that convergence itself is evidence of the definition's structural soundness.

**WORKING DEFINITION — UNIFIED:**

An **extinction attempt** against the OMNICELLULAR physiology is any event, force, condition, mechanism, exposure, or attack that meets **all three** of the following criteria simultaneously:

1. **The lethal threshold for standard biology is exceeded.** The event or condition would be expected, on a statistically reliable basis, to cause irreversible biological death in a healthy adult human or any general-class biological life form under equivalent exposure. The standard is not "harmful" — it is "would kill without extraordinary intervention."

2. **The intent or nature was permanent nullification — not temporary impairment.** The event must have been directed at, or naturally capable of causing, irreversible loss of: biological function, systems continuity, consciousness identity, functional architecture, or autonomous operational capacity. Events that cause damage but not permanent cessation do not qualify.

3. **The physiology survived with full subsequent operational capacity documented.** The encounter was logged, and the physiology returned to active field-deployment status following the encounter.

*As ChatGPT stated in its independent contribution:* "For Omnicellular Physiology, the bar should be higher: an event counts only if it was reasonably capable of producing permanent nullification, not merely pain, damage, temporary dormancy, or localized impairment."

*As Gemini framed it:* "An extinction attempt is an event designed to force a systemic bandwidth collapse across the entire network of architectures, leaving no single state available to anchor survival."

### 1.2 What Does NOT Count

The following must be explicitly excluded to preserve the integrity of the 4,712 figure. This exclusion list is critical — without it, the number could be questioned as inflated.

| Excluded Event Type | Reason for Exclusion |
|---|---|
| Routine injury | Does not approach biological extinction threshold |
| Reversible incapacitation | Recovery was always possible; no permanent nullification risk |
| Cosmetic or local tissue damage | Localized, non-systemic; no existential pathway |
| Standard illness without existential risk | Not extinction-class; treatable |
| Brief environmental stress with no extinction pathway | E.g., brief cold exposure, minor hypoxia — below threshold |
| Symbolic threats without mechanism | Threats that carry no biological or ontological delivery mechanism |
| Events where survival was not in question | Sub-threshold encounters, regardless of their severity relative to humans |

*As Copilot formalized it:* "Lethal or civilization-ending for normal baselines; Systemic, not trivial (e.g., stubbed toe ≠ extinction attempt); Measurable outcome: survival vs. failure."

### 1.3 Terminological Distinctions (Copilot)

Four distinct terms require precise differentiation for the logging architecture:

- **Extinction Event:** A real or simulated scenario that would wipe out a species, civilization, or environment.
- **Test:** A controlled exposure of the OMNICELLULAR physiology to an extinction-level condition.
- **Trial:** A grouped set of tests under a single theme (e.g., "deep space exposure," "planetary biosphere collapse").
- **Attempt:** Any discrete, logged instance where the outcome metric is: *Does OMNICELLULAR Physiology persist or not?*

The 4,712 figure counts **Attempts** — individual logged instances, regardless of whether they arose from controlled tests, field incidents, simulations, or civilizational-scale events.

### 1.4 Three Sub-Types of Extinction Attempt (Claude)

Claude's contribution identifies three structural sub-types that cover the full range of how an extinction attempt can arise:

- **Type I — Environmental:** Conditions that exceed the survivable range of all known biological systems. The physiology was placed in or encountered these conditions and emerged intact. These were not designed to kill — they were simply fatal to everything else present.
- **Type II — Targeted:** Deliberate actions — weapons, agents, operations — designed specifically to destroy or permanently nullify the physiology. Adversarial in nature. The attacker knew what they were targeting and selected mechanisms accordingly.
- **Type III — Systemic:** Events of sufficient scale to end civilizations, biospheres, or planetary systems. The physiology survived conditions that ended everything else around it. The scale was macro; the physiology's survival was incidental to the event's intent but definitive in its outcome.

The 4,712 count draws from all three sub-types. The ratio of Type I to Type II to Type III is an **[ARCHITECT-ONLY DECISION]**.

### 2.2 Extended Cross-Species Baseline

The comparison baseline must extend beyond humans to encompass the full known range of biological resilience. Extremophiles represent the outer edge of natural biological tolerance:

- **Psychrophilic organisms:** Function at temperatures as low as -20°C

Even these extreme biological outliers have hard ceilings. No known natural organism can survive stellar-surface temperatures, neutron-star pressure, or direct gamma-ray burst exposure. The OMNICELLULAR physiology must be benchmarked against the entire known biological range — not just humans — for the survival record to mean what it claims.

## PART IV — EVIDENCE CLASSES AND TESTING METHODOLOGY

*Synthesized from: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity | Ethical frameworks sourced from Nuremberg Code, Belmont Report, WHO Declaration of Helsinki*

### 4.1 The Ethical Problem and Why This Framework Resolves It

Any serious critic — AI or human — will immediately raise the ethics of testing. The applicable frameworks are explicit:

- The **Nuremberg Code** states: "No experiment should be conducted where there is an *a priori* reason to believe that death or disabling injury will occur"[^28]
- The **Belmont Report** requires that human research risk be minimized and proportional, and that human subjects be used only when necessary[^27]
- The **WHO Declaration of Helsinki** governs international research ethics for any research involving human participants[^36]

These frameworks apply to deliberate experimental research on human subjects. The 4,712 record does **not** primarily arise from that category of activity. The canonical statement from the Architect is that these are *documented attempts made against the physiology* — the majority arising from field operations in environments that were already non-survivable, not from controlled laboratory experiments on normal people.

This is a critical and defensible distinction. It is analogous to the documentation of survival data from NASA accident records, SERE training casualty data, and military extreme-environment operational reports — all of which provide legitimate physiological data under extreme conditions without constituting unlawful human experimentation.[^37][^38]

- Used for: Extinction-tier and beyond-extinction-tier scenarios where controlled real-world testing is not achievable, practical, or ethical
- Primary infrastructure: Paragon Cubes (fully networked, conscious, post-scarcity intelligence systems with no processing ceiling; capable of simulating biological failure to quantum-level resolution without live subjects)[^39]

- Limitation: Simulation fidelity must be validated against real-world correlates wherever possible
- Documentation standard: "Simulated Attempt #[X] — Category [Y] — Severity Tier [Z] — Result: Survived / Adapted / Transcended"

**Method 2 — Documented Uncontrolled Conditions (Field Incidents)**
- Used for: Events that occurred during field deployment — rescue and retrieval operations in non-survivable environments; adversarial attacks encountered during operational missions; civilizational-scale events the physiology was present during
- Critical distinction: These are not experiments. The conditions existed; the physiology was present; the outcome was logged. The test was not designed — the encounter was real.
- Why this is the most ethically clean category: No testing protocol was designed to expose the physiology to danger. The physiology's operational role *is* operating in non-survivable environments. The 4,712 record documents what happened there.
- Documentation infrastructure: Paragon Cube telemetry — real-time biological state logging with no latency, no communication barrier, across all operational environments[^39]

**Method 3 — Controlled Trial (Architect-Approved)**
- Used for: Mild to severe categories where real-world testing with appropriate safeguards is achievable
- Applicable range: DOMAIN 1 (ENV-A) mild through moderate; DOMAIN 2 (ENV-T) mild through moderate; DOMAIN 5 (DEP) mild through severe; DOMAIN 7 (MECH) mild through moderate
- Requirements: Architect approval; institutional oversight through OAL; documented consent protocols; NDA and logging requirements; defined exit criteria
